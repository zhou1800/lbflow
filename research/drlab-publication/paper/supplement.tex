Source checksums identify the numerical implementation. The main executable accepts one simulation configuration per MPI

Quantitative results use equal reference densities, unit lattice speed and

\section{Equilibrium and source moments}
For the actual lattice weights,
\begin{align}
 \sum_iw_i&=1,&\sum_iw_i e_{ia}&=0,&\sum_iw_i e_{ia}e_{ib}&=\delta_{ab}/3,\\
 \sum_iw_i e_{ia}e_{ib}e_{ic}e_{id}
 &= (\delta_{ab}\delta_{cd}+\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}+\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc})/9.
\end{align}
Thus the equilibrium has mass $\rho$, momentum $\rho\bm u$, and second moment
$pI+\rho\bm u\bm u$, where
$p=\rho_0c_{p,0}^2+\rho_1c_{p,1}^2$.
The recovered phase weights yield
$c_{p,k}^2=3(1-\alpha_k)/5$ in D2Q9 and $(1-\alpha_k)/2$ in D3Q19.
The reference-density relation between the two alphas balances pure-phase
pressure; it does not establish thermodynamic consistency.

The unprefactored Guo source $G_i$ has
\begin{equation}
 \sum_iG_i=0,\quad \sum_i\bm e_iG_i=\bm F,\quad
 \sum_i\bm e_i\bm e_iG_i=\bm u\bm F+\bm F\bm u.
\end{equation}
These identities are tested against the source emitted by the production
function, at nonzero velocity and force in all coordinate components.
Actual $M$ and $M^{-1}$ are emitted and checked independently.

The force-integration counterexample follows directly from the original
shifted equilibrium. Its momentum increment is $s_j\tau F$.
The old D3Q19 momentum rates were zero; old D2Q9 momentum rates were one.
SRT's $s_j=1/\tau$ gives $F$ instead. The corrected source and half-force
velocity give net $F$ in both collision operators.

\section{Perturbation and recoloring}
The actual perturbation arrays satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \sum_iB_i=1/3,\qquad \sum_i\bm e_iB_i=0,\qquad
 \sum_i\bm e_i\bm e_iB_i=I/3.
\end{equation}
For a unit normal $\bm n$ and
$\Delta_i=9\sigma|\bm g|[w_i(\bm e_i\cdot\bm n)^2-B_i]/(4\tau)$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i\Delta_i=0,\quad \sum_i\bm e_i\Delta_i=0,\quad
 \sum_i\bm e_i\bm e_i\Delta_i=\frac{\sigma|\bm g|}{2\tau}(\bm n\bm n-I).
\end{equation}
The trace is nonzero, so an MRT implementation must account for energy and
other even moments as well as shear. Direct projection of the actual arrays
onto $M$, using 32 deterministic random normals, identifies coupled rows
\{1,2,7,8\} in D2Q9 and \{1,2,9,10,11,12,13,14,15\} in D3Q19
(zero-based indices). All use local rate $1/\tau$. Odd perturbation moments
vanish. Applying an additional Guo source prefactor to this calibrated
perturbation would change its surface tension and is not done.

Opposite directions have opposite recoloring cosine and equal equilibrium
weights at rest. Consequently the recoloring correction sums to zero, and
$f_i^{0,*}+f_i^{1,*}=f_i^{post}$ pointwise. Component momentum can change by
equal and opposite segregation fluxes; total momentum is unchanged.

If the original fixed MRT rate $1/\tau_0$ is combined with a perturbation
scaled by local $1/\tau(\chi)$, its capillary stress has factor
$\tau_0/\tau(\chi)$. A thin-interface estimate with arithmetic interpolation is
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_{eff}\simeq\sigma\tau_0
 \int_{-1}^{1}\frac{d\chi}{2\tau(\chi)}
 =\sigma\frac{\tau_0}{\tau_0-\tau_1}\log\frac{\tau_0}{\tau_1}.
\end{equation}
This is a leading continuum estimate, not an exact finite-grid prediction.
At prescribed $\sigma=0.005$, the original solver gives 0.0031483 for
$(\tau_0,\tau_1)=(0.6,1.4)$ after 40000 steps and 0.0076439 for the opposite
pair after 12000 steps. Relative late pressure changes are
$4.8\times10^{-5}$ and $3.2\times10^{-9}$. The corrected arithmetic cases have
Laplace errors 0.145\% and 0.170\%; the harmonic cases have 0.841\% and
1.159\%. Local relaxation therefore removes the major capillary calibration
defect without proving exact finite-grid viscosity independence.

\section{Time and checkpoint contract}
The integer-time incoming momentum is $\bm j_n=\rho\bm u_n-\bm F/2$.
At physical rest, populations are initialized as
$f_i^{eq}(0)-G_i(0)/2$. The normal collision, perturbation and recoloring
kernel is applied once to create the outgoing state at time zero.
For uniform constant forcing,
\begin{equation}
 \bm j_n=(n-1/2)\bm F,\quad \bm j_n^*=(n+1/2)\bm F,\quad
 \bm u_n=n\bm F/\rho.
\end{equation}
An outgoing checkpoint labelled $n$ is followed by streaming and reporting
at $n+1$. Its physical velocity, if reconstructed directly before streaming,
would use $(\bm j_n^*-\bm F/2)/\rho$, not the incoming-state plus sign.
The sidecar identifies the outgoing stage, lattice, dimensions, phase count,
time and next action. Population-file length is checked. The complete
configuration and geometry are recorded separately in run receipts; the
sidecar does not validate every physical parameter.

Nonfinite outgoing populations abort before checkpoint writing.
Convergence histories reset on restart, so the same later state is
reproduced while an identical automatic stopping time is not promised.
The native-double restart representation is tested on the same Linux
platform; cross-endian portability is not claimed.

\section{References and measurement protocols}
For a force-density-driven channel of wall spacing $H$,
\begin{equation}
 u(y)=\frac{F_x}{2\mu}y(H-y),\quad \overline u=\frac{F_xH^2}{12\mu}.
\end{equation}
Fluid-node coordinates are $y=1/2,3/2,\ldots,H-1/2$ relative to the lower
halfway wall. Force scales as $H^{-3}$ and duration as $H^2$, with fixed
lattice viscosity. This is diffusive refinement of the same physical channel.

For two layers, each solution is $-F_xy^2/(2\mu_k)+a_ky+b_k$.
Four linear conditions enforce two no-slip walls and continuity of velocity
and shear stress. A second reference solves the measured diffuse-viscosity
problem independently:
\begin{equation}
 C=F_x\frac{\int_0^H y/\mu(y)\,dy}{\int_0^H 1/\mu(y)\,dy},\quad
 u(y)=\int_0^y\frac{C-F_xs}{\mu(s)}\,ds.
\end{equation}
Viscosity is interpolated between node centers and numerical quadrature is
performed on 64 subdivisions per lattice spacing. This reference uses the
measured phase profile and is not a manufactured analytic interface profile.

Laplace pressure is compared with $(d-1)\sigma/R$. Subgrid intersections
on all Cartesian edges are fit with orthogonal-distance circle/sphere
regression. Pressure is averaged where $|\chi|>0.99$ and the sampling point
is at least three nodes from the fitted interface. Fit residuals, sample
counts, spurious velocity, component masses and late pressure changes are
retained. The smallest radii are finite-interface tests.

The wetting angle is measured through phase 1 using
\begin{equation}
 \theta=\arccos\frac{y_w-y_c}{R_c},\qquad y_w=0.5.
\end{equation}
The convention is consistent with $\bm n_\chi\cdot\bm n_w=\cos\theta$,
where $\bm n_w$ points away from solid and $\bm n_\chi$ toward phase 0.
Solid-to-fluid contour crossings are excluded. The principal fit uses
$\chi=0$ and wall exclusion $0.75W_{0.9}$; the sensitivity envelope uses
$\chi=-0.1,0,0.1$ and exclusions $0.5,0.75,1,1.5$ interface widths.
Invalid fits are recorded rather than replaced with invented angles.

For a flat wall, weighted stencil reconstruction solves
$A\bm g=\bm b$ with $A=\sum_{fluid}w_i\bm e_i\bm e_i$.
In physical $(x,y)$ coordinates, $A=\operatorname{diag}(5/18,1/6)$, so
the clipped estimator $3\bm b$ has factors $5/6$ and $1/2$.
This linear-field result does not ensure a nonlinear diffuse profile,
halfway-wall placement or surface-energy consistency. The wall normal and
angle rotation are held common in the ablation.

\section{Pore-flow and digital-rock protocols}
Phase-fraction-weighted advective flux is averaged over the complete specimen,
including solid voxels with zero velocity. Component mass is audited separately.
The SCSP convention is $\rho_{\rm ref}\bm u_{\rm ref}=\sum_i\bm e_i f_i+
\bm F/2$, with reference-density equilibrium. ColorFluid reconstructs
$\rho\bm u=\sum_i\bm e_i(f_i^0+f_i^1)+\bm F/2$. At unit reference density a
pure-phase velocity comparison uses $\bm u_{\rm ref}/\rho$. It is not expected
to be raw-velocity equality in a nonuniform pressure field. The pressure
report now displays $\Delta\rho/3$ as $\Delta p$; the previous factor three
was included in the single-phase permeability prefactor. The reported
permeability is algebraically unchanged at unit reference density. An
independent pressure-driven slit checks both the displayed pressure and $K$.

For a slit of $H$ fluid nodes with physical halfway walls, midpoint sampling
gives
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{j=0}^{H-1}(j+\tfrac12)(H-j-\tfrac12)=H^3/6+H/12.
\end{equation}
Therefore a pore occupying a specimen of total height $N_y$ has
$q=F(H^3/12+H/24)/(\nu N_y)$. Two isolated pores of widths $8s$ and $16s$
occupy a specimen of height $36s$. Their single-phase baseline is
$K_{\rm grid}=\sum_k(H_k^3/12+H_k/24)/(36s)$, and
$k_{r,k}=\nu_kq_k/(FK_{\rm grid})$ equals each pore's fraction of this
conductance. Three resolutions, two lattices and the two viscosity permutations
give 18 executions including single-phase baselines. This normalization is
verified in disconnected pure-component pores, not as a general
relative-permeability curve with shared interfaces.

The entry controls impose density $1+3\Delta p$ at the left boundary and 1
at the right, with component 0 supplied on the left and component 1 on the
right. The solid value is a prescribed 60-degree angle through component 1.
The interior equivalent invasion length is
$L_0=\sum_{\rm interior}\varphi_0/H^{d-1}$; boundary planes and solid rows
are excluded. The late change in this quantity checks retreat below and
advance above the selected sharp static reference. These are open systems;
they are not included in the closed-mass audit.

The hydraulic diagnostic uses $p_c^{app}=\Delta p-\bar u/C$.
For a slit $C=(H^2+1/2)/(12\mu L)$ accounts for sampled Poiseuille flow;
for a square tube the continuum coefficient is
\begin{equation}
 C=\frac{H^2}{12\mu L}
 \left[1-\frac{192}{\pi^5}\sum_{n=1,3,\ldots}
 \frac{\tanh(n\pi/2)}{n^5}\right].
\end{equation}
The series is evaluated through $n=399$. This inference does not remove
contact-line resistance or voxel-corner effects. Its difference from a sharp
static pressure is a diagnostic, not a measured dynamic contact angle or
an error against a complete moving-meniscus solution. Twelve slit executions
cover three diffusive resolutions, both gradients and both pressure ratios;
four square-tube executions exercise the three-dimensional wall operator.

\section{Diffuse transport derivation and additional measurements}
For $\mu u'=C-Fy$ with no-slip walls, $C=FI_1/I_0$ follows from
$\int_0^H u'\,dy=0$. Interchanging the integrations in
$Q=\int_0^H\int_0^y(C-Fz)/\mu(z)\,dz\,dy$ gives
$Q=F(I_2-I_1^2/I_0)$. This is a continuum identity for a specified viscosity
profile. It does not presume a material interface or a new closure.

Put $A=1/\mu_0$, $B=1/\mu_1$, $c=(A+B)/2$, $d=(B-A)/2$,
and $s=y-H/2$. Define centered moments $J_n=\int s^n/\mu(s)\,ds$.
The conductance is invariant to this shift:
$Q/F=J_2-J_1^2/J_0$. For the centered sharp step,
\begin{equation}
 J_0=cH,\qquad J_1=dH^2/4,\qquad J_2=cH^3/12,\qquad
 Q_{sharp}/F=H^3(4c^2-3d^2)/(48c).
\end{equation}
For arithmetic interpolation of the tanh phase fraction, integration on the
real line gives
\begin{equation}
 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac1{\mu_A(s)}-
 \frac1{\mu_{sharp}(s)}\right)ds=\xi d\ln(\mu_1/\mu_0).
\end{equation}
The leading moment corrections are
$\delta J_0=\xi d\ln r$, $\delta J_1=O(\xi^2)$ and
$\delta J_2=O(\xi^3)$. Expanding $-J_1^2/J_0$ gives the arithmetic
coefficient in the main paper. Harmonic interpolation instead has
$1/\mu_H=c+d\tanh(s/\xi)$, for which the excess $J_0$ vanishes by symmetry
and $J_2$ is unchanged. Using
$\int_0^\infty s[1-\tanh(s/\xi)]ds=\pi^2\xi^2/24$ gives
\begin{equation}
 J_1=d\left(H^2/4-\pi^2\xi^2/12\right)
\end{equation}
up to exponentially small boundary tails, yielding the quadratic coefficient.
At viscosity ratio 9:1, the leading coefficients in powers of $\xi/H$ are
$-1.62256584$ and $+6.07360271$. Independent continuum quadrature at
$\xi/H=0.01,0.005,0.0025$ approaches these limits. These synthetic quadrature
checks are analytic verification, not additional simulator executions.

The additional campaign contains three planar and eighteen layered D2Q9/MRT
executions, with $\beta\in\{0.35,0.7,0.9\}$ and $H\in\{16,32,64\}$.
The 9:1 viscosity ratio uses $(\tau_0,\tau_1)=(0.6,1.4)$.
Planar cases run 8000 steps; layered runs cover at least twenty slow
viscous decay times. Their longest duration is 250000 steps. Reference
profiles are sampled at the same halfway-wall cell centers as the measured
velocity; continuous volume flux is retained separately.
The tanh prediction uses only independent planar width calibration.
The measured-diffuse reference interpolates the observed phase fractions,
linearly with constant continuation from the first/last fluid node to the
wall, using 256 quadrature intervals per lattice cell. It is an explanatory
reference, not an independent prediction of the phase profile. A separate
postprocessing calculation holds the observed samples fixed and compares
linear interpolation at 1024 intervals per cell with shape-preserving PCHIP
interpolation at the same quadrature density. The resulting discrepancy
envelopes in \texttt{data/reference-sensitivity.json} describe the specified
reconstructions; they do not bound every admissible phase profile or isolate
lattice error. The largest linear quadrature refinement change is
$9.12\times10^{-8}$ relative to the measured velocity. The largest
linear-to-PCHIP reference change is 0.527\%.

All late relative velocity changes are below $9.65\times10^{-12}$ and the
maximum relative component/total mass drift is $9.11\times10^{-12}$.
Fluid densities range from 0.9999955 to 1.0000318 across this campaign.
These small departures from the unit-density continuum approximation are
recorded rather than erased. Complete profiles, force/surface-tension scaling,
reference errors, inputs and executable hashes are in
\texttt{data/interfacial-transport.json}. Every measured record was reproduced
from its raw fields, and input, geometry and frozen-executable hashes were
verified. The calculation uses the existing operators; no numerical kernel

\section{Neutral-wall phase balance and adjacent-time controls}
The new \texttt{data/wall-consistency.json} retains the independent phase
recurrence, flat-wall zero/nonzero-sigma controls, rotation/parity tests,
manufactured fixed points, adjacent physical fields, matched bulk drops
and nonneutral negative controls. Coordinate/diagonal contributions to a
bulk tangential phase update are $A=a+2b$ and $B=a+\sqrt{2}b$; local reflection
leaves $A_w=a+b$ and $B_w=a+b/\sqrt{2}$. Scaling the two wall-parallel
coordinate recoloring terms by the factor $k$ derived in the main text
sets $B_w/A_w=B/A$. The matched residual is $5/6$ of the bulk residual;
this is a stationary-profile condition, not equality of transient transport.
The factor applies to opposite links symmetrically and leaves total
populations unchanged. Neutral tangent-magnitude reconstruction is also
required at degenerate extrema: an odd-grid manufactured control without
it fails. Its frozen source is reconstructible with
\texttt{run-kernels/wall-pre-neutral-norm.patch} and the accompanying hashes.

Manufactured tests use an independently relaxed scalar bulk profile to
construct outgoing populations, rather than reading an equilibrium from
the hydrodynamic solver. With no flow and unit total density,
$f_{0i}^{out}=w_i[\varphi+\beta\varphi(1-\varphi)
 (\bm e_i\cdot\bm n)/|\bm e_i|]$ for moving directions and
$f_{00}^{out}=w_0\varphi$; the second phase is complementary.
The tangent multiplier is included only at wall-adjacent coordinate links.
Both 64- and 65-cell periodic tangential domains are tested at three
segregation strengths and both curvature forms with MRT; a rotated control
uses SRT. Seeds, native-double outgoing metadata and checksums are retained.
MPI and restart comparisons are bitwise identical.

The gradient curvature form differentiates neighboring $\bm g$ without
normalizing each neighbor. Its local force uses the same $10^{-9}$ mask,
while weak neighboring raw gradients remain in the derivative.
The continuously equivalent force expressions have established precedents
\cite{sugimoto2026}. Matched 44/80-cell square drop domains separate the
force-stencil comparison from two additional 48/96-cell domain controls.
These controls do not establish higher-density or three-dimensional wall
accuracy.

Staggered amplitudes use the fluid-node mean of $\rho u_x(-1)^x$ and
$\rho u_y(-1)^y$. Projection energy and raw velocities are retained alongside
one- and two-step differences. Their adjacent-step average is a diagnostic;
no averaging or mode removal enters collision or streaming. The relevant
dispersion mode has established precedents \cite{lallemand2000,guo2011}.
Complete nonlinear equilibrium checkerboard seeds of amplitude $10^{-6}$
are propagated with SCSP and color, SRT and MRT, under even reports.
The new adjacent-step criterion prevents premature termination, whereas
stationary controls retain termination after three stable report intervals.
The scalar and velocity comparisons use the existing tolerances.
An audit may inspect root-local force/gradient arrays from an identified
outgoing state with \texttt{NumericalAudit input.prm --capillary-state};
local size, global size and local origin are explicitly identified.

The neutral correction is not validated for nonneutral wetting. Direct
60/120-degree pressure controls use the same three-width scaling as the
earlier study; 60-degree normal-CSF cases are held to four initial durations,
with actual sample intervals retained. Their remaining pressure bias is
larger than in the uncorrected reconstructed-CSF controls. These results
preclude a claim of a generally accurate capillary boundary or trapping law.

\section{Additional wall-transport and location controls}
The distributed \texttt{data/wall-flux.json} recomputes all included raw
measurements and checksums. A relative-phase face transfer is applied after
bounce-back and before the macroscopic update. Outgoing phase fractions
sum all nine populations, including rest, before streaming; saved restart
populations retain their outgoing meaning. Diagonal phase/total fields and
fractions have their own halo exchange. Complementary phase assignment
retains each original total incoming population. Paired face transfers
conserve component mass globally, rather than preserving each node's
component mass separately.

For a neutral one-dimensional phase profile at rest, the replaced blocked
diagonals add $b\Delta_x\varphi$ and
$\beta b(J_{j-1}-J_{j+1})/\sqrt{2}$ to the wall scalar update. Therefore
$A_w+b=A$ and $B_w+b/\sqrt{2}=B$. This is complete transient agreement;
the earlier tangent multiplier only gives a matched stationary ratio.
It is the established specular-forward scalar mechanism
\cite{ginzburg2015}, embedded here in a total-population-preserving phase
transfer. The face fraction average and its nonneutral extension are
explicit closures. No general positivity, energy-dissipation or dynamic
contact-line theorem is asserted.

Independent tests cover lengths 64/65, beta values 0.35/0.7/0.9, random
mixed fractions and nonuniform hydrodynamic equilibria. Six full-solver
zero-sigma controls write every time step from 0 through 64, and twelve
nonzero-sigma controls exercise the halfway/ghost gradients, both collision
operators and all three beta values at odd length. Independently generated
equilibria omit the older tangent multiplier. Pure forced phases remain
bitwise unchanged with phase transfer. Nonneutral short-time MPI/restart
checks compare both macroscopic fields and every outgoing population byte.
Rotation is checked separately; an outgoing-state gradient audit compares
the ghost implementation with an independent full-grid calculation.
The six CTest groups retain the earlier default and checkerboard controls.

The halfway cosine control uses an interior unprojected gradient with the
unchanged $10^{-9}$ mask. Below that cutoff it explicitly continues the
target cosine. It preserves the first-row central tangent derivative and
requires a nonsingular angle and an interior fluid layer. Only the inward
component of the extrapolated unit normal is constrained; a general
normalized-vector extrapolation is not implied.
For the published ghost-color comparison \cite{liu2015}, row-wise central
tangent differences are extrapolated to the wall before constructing the
virtual solid color. All color-stencil entries are then included without
angle rotation. Prepared row derivatives and fluid-associated ghost values
are exchanged to avoid requiring a second halo layer. The cited formula
is reproduced with this paper's phase convention, but the remaining
recoloring and MRT operators retain LBFlow's definitions.

Each method uses the same equal reference densities, tau=1, input sigma,
physical halfway walls and bulk recoloring widths. The H16 comparison
contains seven angles at beta=0.7, plus three beta values at 60/90/120
degrees for the ghost control. The three-width 60-degree comparisons use
four initial durations. Pressure is sampled directly using the buffers
and color cutoffs stated above. Circle fits retain exclusions of
0.5/0.75/1 measured bulk widths; these extrapolations are sensitivity
diagnostics, not direct independent contact-angle measurements.

The apparent near-zero H16 halfway/bounce-back pressure error is not
preserved at H32/H48. Ghost continuation with phase transfer improves the
tested 60-degree pressure sequence but retains nonneutral currents and
angle dependence. Even-time late color changes alone can hide two-step
oscillations. The H16 60-degree ghost/transfer control has one-step maximum
color change $6.65\times10^{-7}$ and two-step change
$3.54\times10^{-12}$, while its raw/adjacent-averaged maximum speeds remain
approximately $4.18\times10^{-5}$ and $4.27\times10^{-5}$.
These are pressure-stable finite-time controls, not complete mechanical
equilibria. The supplied adjacent-step diagnostics preserve raw
velocities; they do not hide these currents with temporal averaging.
Corners, staircase or curved walls, open reservoirs and selective films
are explicitly rejected for the experimental flat-wall operators. These
static tests do not establish a quantitative invasion/retraction interval.

\section{Scientific limits and publication status}
Only equal-density, low-speed flow in the documented parameter range is
verified. Curved-wall and three-dimensional wetting accuracy, contact-line
dynamics, hysteresis, high density ratio, arbitrary pressure weights, and
steady rock phase-specific Darcy permeability are not established. The
isolated-pore normalization is independently verified within its stated geometry. The two viscosity
rules represent alternative finite-interface closures. The geometrical
projection and weighted linear reconstruction are established ideas;
the ablation does not demonstrate superiority over all published wetting
schemes or a new bulk model.
