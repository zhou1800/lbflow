\section{Discrete interface kinetics and capillary resistance}
\label{sec:phasekinetics}
\subsection{Width and leading diffusion do not specify shape relaxation}
The fixed-width resistance trend requires a kinetic control independent of
the momentum balance. For the rest-state projection stencil, its leading
bulk equation is the known conservative Allen--Cahn form
\cite{subhedar2020,mirjalili2020}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leadingphase}
 \partial_t\varphi+\nabla\cdot(\varphi\bm u)
 =D\nabla\cdot[\nabla\varphi-2b\varphi(1-\varphi)\bm n],
 \qquad \bm n=\frac{\nabla\varphi}{|\nabla\varphi|}.
\end{equation}
Here $D=1/6$ and $b=\beta/h$ in the leading diffusive scaling;
$h=16/H$ denotes the grid spacing in the H16 length units.
The mapping is established, and does not supply a chemical-potential
mobility or a calibrated contact-line law.

For a smooth signed-distance field $d$, with $|\nabla d|=1$ in the
interface neighborhood, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distanceflux}
 \varphi=\frac{1+\tanh(bd)}{2},\qquad
 \nabla\varphi=2b\varphi(1-\varphi)\nabla d.
\end{equation}
The leading nonadvective flux vanishes pointwise, including for curved
signed-distance contours. This familiar profile-preservation property
\cite{mirjalili2020,hao2024} does not say that every discrete representation
has zero shape relaxation. In a linearization about a planar profile,
the normal must vary with the phase perturbation. Freezing the normal
would introduce an erroneous leading tangential-diffusion term.

We differentiate the complete zero-flow projection recurrence, including
the unit-normal variation and production color-gradient cutoff, at its
independently equilibrated planar profile. Let $T(q)$ be its transverse
Fourier matrix and $\mu(q)$ its eigenvalue with greatest overlap with
planar translation. The parameter-free prediction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phaserate}
 \Gamma(q)=-\frac{\log\mu(q)}{h^2},\qquad
 q=\frac{m\pi}{H},\qquad k_{phys}=\frac{m\pi}{16}.
\end{equation}
The matrix is given in the supplement. Its action agrees with the
finite-difference derivative of the nonlinear scalar stencil within
$\PhaseSymbolError$. No measured decay rate enters this calculation.

Fourteen complete LBM runs apply paired positive and negative transverse
displacements of magnitude 0.01 to a neutral planar bridge; the coarse
grid additionally uses magnitude 0.02. Both interfaces in each bridge
share the same axial displacement. Both force and surface tension are
zero. The first mode is measured at $H=16,32,48$ and a second-wavelength
control at $H=16,32$. Late projected-amplitude decay rates agree with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:phaserate} within \PhaseSpectrumPercent\%
(Fig.~\ref{fig:phasespectrum}). First-mode rates are
$6.0954\times10^{-5}$, $1.5552\times10^{-5}$ and
$6.9031\times10^{-6}$ in physical time units.
The second/first rate ratios are approximately 16.18 and 15.97.
The nearly constant $\Gamma/(h^2 k_{phys}^4)$, approximately 0.041--0.042,
is consistent with fourth-order numerical shape relaxation in this
long-wavelength test. A formal isolated-interface expansion in the
supplement gives $\Gamma=h^2k_{phys}^4/24+O(h^4)$ when tails and the
cutoff do not intervene. This conditional limit is not a uniform
arbitrary-interface coefficient. Exact finite-grid eigenvalues,
rather than that expansion, predict the measured rates.

\subsection{An explicit hydraulic control and independent Stokes reference}
To distinguish hydrodynamic wall regularization from phase kinetics, we
add an optional prescribed Navier slip length for neutral flat D2Q9 walls
at equal $\tau=1$, projection recoloring and legacy MRT.
Bounce-back/specular mixtures are established slip controls
\cite{ahmed2009}; this option is not a new boundary concept.
With lattice slip $\lambda_l=\lambda/h$, the specular fraction is
$s=\lambda_l/(\lambda_l+1/2)$. Specular populations come from the forward
tangential neighbor, and the existing relative-phase transfer receives
factor $1-s$. This preserves the zero-flow neutral phase recurrence
without double-counting its tangential transport. The default $\lambda=0$
retains bitwise-identical fields and outgoing populations in three
closed-kernel comparisons.

Eighteen signed pure-channel controls over three heights and three slip
lengths verify
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slippure}
 u_j=\frac{F_l}{2\nu_l}
 [y_j(H-y_j)+\lambda_l H],\qquad y_j=j+\tfrac12.
\end{equation}
Neutral equilibrium, component conservation, rotation, two-rank exchange
and outgoing restart are checked separately. Nonneutral, unequal-tau and
pressure-reservoir uses of the slip option are rejected.

An independent staggered finite-volume calculation solves constant-viscosity
Stokes flow through a Fourier/biharmonic streamfunction and evolves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:leadingphase} conservatively. It uses a logistic-distance
reconstruction for the phase flux and capillary geometry. Signed-distance
reconstruction is established \cite{hao2024}; our implementation is a
separately verified numerical reference, not a replication of that
paper's complete algorithm. The supplemental analytic and manufactured
checks establish second-order hydraulic accuracy, cancellation of pure
gradient forcing, planar phase equilibrium, prescribed scalar translation
and zero-sigma hydraulic decoupling. The reference's pure-channel
sampling offset $h^2/4$ is retained explicitly.

The common physical domain is height 16 and periodic length 64, with
$W_{0.9}=4.765235$, $\nu=D=1/6$, $\sigma=0.005$, $F=10^{-7}$ and
prescribed hydraulic slip $\lambda=1$. Two interfaces span the channel,
and their circular phase centroid measures displacement. At the final
sampled states of nominal physical duration 8000, LBM and independent-reference mean speeds differ by
17.12\%, 15.18\% and \PhaseFineFVDifference\% over the three grids.
This comparison is an unresolved discrepancy, not successful independent
dynamic validation. Doubling the H16/H48 LBM durations changes late
centroid speed by at most \PhaseDurationPercent\%.
An H16 signed force-doubling pair remains linear within $3\times10^{-7}$
in relative mean mobility. Reversing the force and halving the H32
reference time step changes its speed magnitude by $\PhaseFVTimeSignError$
relative to the positive-force full-step result. These controls exclude
the tested time/rate effects as explanations of the much larger discrepancy.
Swapping the H32 initial equilibria between the finite-volume and
shared-phase algorithms changes their late mean speeds by less than
$2\times10^{-6}$ relatively. The tested initialization difference does
not explain the dynamic discrepancy either.

A separate control increases the prescribed physical slip to $\lambda=4$
without changing width, force or the numerical kernel. Six signed pure
profiles verify Eq.~\eqref{eq:slippure} within $\PhaseLargerSlipPureError$
relatively. The three dynamic mean-speed differences decrease to 5.40\%,
3.95\% and \PhaseLargerSlipSpeedPercent\%. This is a sensitivity result,
not independent validation. Define the equivalent hydraulic resistance
$R=F/\overline u$ and its excess relative to each analytic discrete pure
channel, $R_{ex}=R-R_0$. At H48, $R_{ex}/R_0$ is 0.12735 for LBM and
0.16407 for FV: their normalized excesses still differ by
\PhaseLargerSlipExcessPercent\%. Larger pure conductance partly dilutes the
relative speed gap. The prescribed-slip table in the supplement retains
both slip controls, so a smaller speed percentage cannot be mistaken for
converged capillary resistance.

\subsection{Separating momentum, force and phase operators}
The actual production capillary force is reconstructed from two adjacent
outgoing states, with color and density matched to their physical fields.
After averaging cell forces onto staggered faces, the independent
Stokes solve predicts the measured LBM mean speed within 0.493\%,
0.134\% and \PhaseFineHydraulicDifference\%.
Replacing this force by the independent capillary formula on the
\emph{same} phase field also yields differences below 1\%.
The flow and force discrepancies under these specific audits are much
smaller than the complete dynamic-reference discrepancy.

We then retain the exact zero-flow projection phase stencil while keeping
the independent Stokes solver, conservative face advection and reference
capillary force. This is an operator ablation: it shares phase kinetics
with LBFlow and cannot serve as independent continuum validation.
Its mean speeds differ from LBM by 1.166\%, 0.489\% and
\PhaseFineHybridDifference\%; the phase-centroid differences are
1.166\%, 0.489\% and 0.292\%.
Halving the H32 ablation time step changes the mean speed by
$\PhaseHybridTimeError$ relatively.
Table~\ref{tab:kineticspeeds} and Fig.~\ref{fig:kineticresistance}
retain all four comparisons.

The combined spectrum prediction and operator ablation identify discrete
phase kinetics as a material contributor to this numerical capillary
response. Matching interface width, leading diffusion and a measured
hydraulic slip condition does not match the complete interface dynamics.
Neither the three-grid reference discrepancy nor the retained-stencil
agreement proves a converged moving-contact-line law. The inference is
bounded to neutral flat walls and low-speed equal-viscosity flow; it does
not quantify nonneutral or curved-rock contact-line physics.
