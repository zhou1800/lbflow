\title{Finite-interface transport and wetting effects in pore-scale lattice Boltzmann simulations with LBFlow}
\author{Yu Zhou\corref{cor1}}
\address{Independent Researcher}
\ead{[private contact omitted]}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\begin{abstract}
Finite numerical interfaces and wall treatment can change pore-scale
transport despite stable, mass-conserving two-phase simulations.
We use LBFlow, a recovered C++/MPI color-gradient lattice Boltzmann program,
to distinguish diffuse constitutive bias, core-operator effects and wetting
response. An inverse-viscosity conductance relation and eighteen layered-flow
controls separate continuum transition effects from discrepancies against
reconstructed references. At viscosity ratio 9:1, the thickest tested
arithmetic and harmonic interfaces change flux by $-9.19$\% and $+21.59$\%,
respectively.
A discrete wall recurrence identifies a stationary imbalance specific to
cosine-normalized recoloring. Published projection recoloring already
preserves that stationary ratio; exact axial transport matching permits an
independent comparison. Conservative relative-phase wall transfer restores
the complete neutral transient. Signed nonneutral pressure, raw parasitic
flow and an established alternating lattice mode remain separately measured.
The published MRT spectrum also changes pure-channel hydraulic response.
Guarded pressure reservoirs then connect bidirectional front drift to closed
capillary pressure, with independently checked component link-flux budgets.
Estimated zero-drift pressure agrees with closed pressure within
\CoreMaxZeroClosedPercent\% of the Young reference, while front mobility is
11.3--17.8\% below separately measured pure-channel mobility. A zero-sigma
control recovers the pure-channel response; a general contact-line law is
not established.
Independent LBPM comparisons on a public Bentheimer specimen check the
single-phase geometric baseline. Two-phase rock cases retain their measured
transient and resolution limits. Recorded inputs, source kernels and
MPI/restart checks support reproduction. A new bulk multiphase model,
curved-rock wetting accuracy and pore-throat trapping thresholds are not
claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and significance}
Pore-scale two-phase flow is controlled by viscous momentum transport,
capillary stress and fluid--solid wetting. A diffuse numerical interface
spreads these processes over several grid nodes. When that width approaches
a pore dimension, a regularization chosen for computation can become part
of the measured flow response. The physical question addressed here is how
to distinguish that constitutive change from a remaining discretization
error, and why an accurate static contact angle alone is insufficient to
verify capillary displacement.

This question matters when simulations connect fluid arrangements in
segmented rock images with transport properties. Geometry, wetting and
averaging conventions already affect that connection; image-based flow
benchmarks emphasize matching the computational domain and observable
\cite{saxena2017}. Recent work measures the influence of pore-size-dependent
wetting on relative permeability \cite{yu2025}. Resolving numerical interface
effects is necessary before assigning a computed response to such material
physics, although the present study does not reproduce those experiments.

Long-lived numerical software can continue producing finite fields after
changes in physical configuration expose an implementation defect. In
multiphase lattice Boltzmann calculations, conservation alone does not verify
force integration, viscosity, surface tension or wetting. The restored
reference solver implementation provides a case in which these contracts can be
inspected separately and then exercised on a public porous specimen.

The historical implementation combines color-gradient perturbation and
recoloring with geometrical wall treatment. These mechanisms belong to the
established color-gradient family \cite{reis2007,latva2005,liu2012,liuerratum2012}.
Direct and modified-gradient contact-angle treatments already appear in
two- and three-dimensional work \cite{leclaire2016,leclaire2017,xu2017,akai2018}.
Later comparisons and curved-wall developments continue to examine
wetting accuracy \cite{sedahmed2024,bhattacharya2026}. A recent review already
compares viscosity, gradient and forcing choices \cite{zahid2025}; a 2026
preprint relates interfacial motion to curvature and additional phase fluxes
\cite{entwistle2026}. Interface sensitivity, harmonic viscosity and geometric
wetting are consequently established subjects. Our analysis uses a simple
continuum conductance relation to separate the physical response of a
diffuse constitutive profile from the error in solving it, then tests the
result over controlled interface and pore-width sequences. Neither this
elementary continuum relation nor the existing interpolation schemes are
claimed as new fundamental laws.

Directly relevant diffuse-interface analyses already address this separation.
Lai et al. derive layered Poiseuille solutions for several viscosity rules
and warn against interpreting diffuse-model error as numerical error
\cite{lai2025}. Our conductance identity is a compact consequence of the
same momentum problem; this separation is not a new concept.
Fuster and Mimoh analyze one-fluid regularization errors \cite{fuster2024},
while Reder et al. derive viscous-stress approximations from mechanical jump
conditions \cite{reder2024}. Their work also cautions against extending a
scalar viscosity comparison to arbitrary multidimensional stress fields.
The present scope is a reproducible diagnosis of these effects in the
recovered implementation and a bounded capillary-operator comparison.
Boundary-induced changes in scalar transport already have analytical
precedents \cite{ginzburg2015}, and color-gradient wetting has been designed
to eliminate interfacial force at a contact line \cite{yu2018}.
Our wall correction is consequently assessed as a specific discrete
fixed-point condition, rather than a new boundary-layer concept.
Color-conserving wetting closures also predate this work
\cite{hollis2011,liu2015,yu2018}. We compare the published flat-wall
ghost-color formula independently of phase reflection. Complex and
three-dimensional curved wetting, including dynamic and trapping tests,
already have dedicated color-gradient developments
\cite{wang2024complex,wang2024curved}. The present flat-wall controls do
not reproduce their full algorithms or establish comparable scope.
Recent phase-field calculations also compare discretizations of
continuously equivalent surface forces \cite{sugimoto2026}; rewriting a
curvature expression does not establish a new force theory.

The source baseline is the reference solver, historical revision
\texttt{1bef9bbe7047}. The full source identity is recorded in the supplement. The original archive is
preserved; repairs, inputs, run receipts and analysis are maintained in a
maintained LBFlow implementation. Dates in commit messages establish source
history but are not treated as evidence of scientific priority.

LBPM already supplies extensive digital-rock multiphase protocols, including
morphological preparation and capillary/relative-permeability workflows
\cite{mcclure2021}. OpenLB offers a broader LBM framework \cite{krause2021};
its current releases include vectorized CPU and GPU execution
\cite{openlb2025}. LBFlow supplies a compact, inspectable implementation for
the present controlled transport and wall-stencil analysis, together with
the recorded digital-rock application. Its independent single-phase
comparison tests the geometric baseline. The paper does not claim
performance superiority over those frameworks, the first digital-rock
simulator or a new bulk multiphase theory. Additional pore-throat trapping
research remains separate from the completed transport results.

\section{Governing model and implementation}
\subsection{Architecture and workflow}
The code separates lattice/grid geometry, MPI exchange, parameter and field
I/O, and fluid collision operators. A byte-valued voxel file assigns initial
phases and solid or boundary labels. A parameter file selects the lattice,
relaxation operator, relaxation times, forcing and wetting. D2Q9 and D3Q19,
SRT and raw-moment MRT, and the single-component and color-fluid executables
are exercised here as configurations of the same executable. The Shan--Chen
placeholder is rejected explicitly.

Directional populations reside in arrays; streaming exchanges the required
neighbor data between MPI subdomains. Macroscopic fields are written as VTI
pieces that the analysis reconstructs into a complete global lattice.
Restart files contain outgoing populations and a sidecar recording the time,
lattice, phase count and next streaming action. Historical checkpoints
without an identifiable stage are rejected. A fresh convergence history is
required after restart. Exceptions and invalid populations cause a nonzero
MPI failure status.

The reproducibility scripts generate every geometry, record inputs and
executable hashes, preserve raw output and calculate independent reference
solutions. Each study uses a frozen executable so a later build cannot alter
an ongoing batch. Algebraic checks are available through CTest.
The public-rock preparation additionally pins the source DOI, data license,
checksums, crop coordinates, voxel scale and periodic connectivity. Numerical
timers include exchange and finite checks, while field/diagnostic output and
checkpoint I/O are counted separately.

\subsection{Recovered and corrected operators}
In lattice units, the component densities and color field are
\begin{equation}
 \rho_k=\sum_i f_i^k,\quad \rho=\rho_0+\rho_1,\quad
 \chi=(\rho_0-\rho_1)/\rho,\quad
 \rho\vct u=\sum_i\vct e_i(f_i^0+f_i^1)+\vct F/2.
\end{equation}
Here $\vct F$ is force per unit volume. The mixture equilibrium is
\begin{equation}
 f_i^{eq}=\rho_0\phi_i^0+\rho_1\phi_i^1+
 \rho w_i[3\vct e_i\!\cdot\!\vct u+
 4.5(\vct e_i\!\cdot\!\vct u)^2-1.5|\vct u|^2].
\end{equation}
The phase weights give $c_{p,k}^2=3(1-\alpha_k)/5$ in D2Q9 and
$(1-\alpha_k)/2$ in D3Q19. The present quantitative tests use equal reference
densities and the standard pressure weights, so $p=\rho/3$.

The legacy arithmetic rule interpolates $\tau$ by component density fraction.
For $\varphi=\rho_0/\rho$ and $\nu_k=(\tau_k-1/2)/3$, the optional harmonic
kinematic-viscosity rule is
\begin{equation}
 \nu^{-1}=\varphi/\nu_0+(1-\varphi)/\nu_1,\qquad \tau=1/2+3\nu.
\end{equation}
The two rules are compared at equal density; equivalence to a dynamic-viscosity
law is not assumed at unequal density.

With $\vct m=M\vct f$, collision and body-force insertion become
\begin{equation}
 \vct m^*=\vct m-S(\vct m-\vct m^{eq})+(I-S/2)M\vct G,
 \quad G_i=w_i[3(\vct e_i-\vct u)+9(\vct e_i\!\cdot\!\vct u)\vct e_i]\!\cdot\!\vct F.
\end{equation}
This uses established discrete forcing and moment relaxation
\cite{guo2002,lallemand2000}. Conserved rates are zero. The retained
nonconserved rates are $s_u=1/\tau$ and $s_q=8(2-s_u)/(8-s_u)$, with
$(1/s_u-1/2)(1/s_q-1/2)=3/16$ \cite{ginzburg2003}.

Interfacial perturbation and recoloring retain the recovered structure:
\begin{align}
 \Delta_i&=\frac{9\sigma |\vct g|}{4\tau}
 \left[w_i(\vct e_i\!\cdot\!\vct n)^2-B_i\right],\quad
 \vct n=\vct g/|\vct g|,\\
 f_i^{0,*}&=\frac{\rho_0}{\rho}f_i^{post}+
 \beta\frac{\rho_0\rho_1}{\rho^2}\cos\varphi_i f_i^{eq}(\vct u=0),\qquad
 f_i^{1,*}=f_i^{post}-f_i^{0,*}.
\end{align}
The actual arrays satisfy zero perturbation mass and momentum and
$\sum_i\vct e_i\vct e_i\Delta_i=\sigma|\vct g|(\vct n\vct n-I)/(2\tau)$.
Every nonzero capillary moment is coupled to a mode with rate $s_u$.

Initialization constructs incoming rest populations with $-\vct G/2$ and
applies the normal collision kernel once. The stored state is then outgoing
at time zero. After streaming, a uniform forced domain has physical velocity
$\vct u(t)=t\vct F/\rho$. The same state convention is used for routine and
convergence-triggered checkpoints.

\subsection{Wetting and termination}
At a wall-adjacent fluid node, the historical gradient skips solid neighbors.
Writing $\mathcal F$ for the remaining stencil,
\begin{equation}
 \vct b=\sum_{i\in\mathcal F}w_i\vct e_i(\chi_i-\chi),\quad
 A=\sum_{i\in\mathcal F}w_i\vct e_i\vct e_i,
 \quad\vct g_{legacy}=3\vct b,\quad\vct g_{ren}=A^{-1}\vct b.
\end{equation}
The second expression is a standard weighted linear reconstruction, not a
new wetting model. It agrees with the bulk stencil and exactly reproduces
linear fields when $A$ has full rank. Unsupported rank-deficient stencils
are rejected. Both variants apply the same recovered angle rotation and
wall normal. On a flat lower wall, the legacy stencil scales the tangential
and normal derivatives by $5/6$ and $1/2$ respectively.

Steady termination requires finite state, stable requested scalar diagnostics,
velocity convergence and, for color flow, convergence of the spatial color
field, for three successive reports. Both report-to-report and adjacent-step
changes must pass; an even reporting interval cannot certify a period-two
oscillation as stationary. Constant saturation cannot terminate a
translating phase field. Periodic acceleration benchmarks disable steady
stopping; undefined pressure-based permeability is identified separately
from invalid numerical state.

\subsection{Experimental capillary operators}
Two optional controls examine limitations exposed by the static tests.
The first fits $\psi=\operatorname{atanh}(\chi)$ on available fluid neighbors
with the same moment tensor $A$, continues $\tanh(\psi+\vct e_i\cdot\nabla\psi)$
over a virtual full stencil, then applies the common angle rotation.
Color is clipped to $\pm(1-10^{-8})$ for this transform. Equilibrium-profile
continuation has prior work \cite{bhattacharya2026}; this local fit is not
an exact implementation of that paper's global ghost-node scheme.

The second replaces perturbation with established continuum surface forcing
\cite{lishchuk2003,sedahmed2024}:
\begin{equation}
 \vct F_s=\frac{\sigma}{2}\kappa\vct g,\qquad
 \kappa=-(I-\vct n\vct n):\nabla\vct n,\quad
 \vct n=\vct g/|\vct g|.
\end{equation}
Normal derivatives use the weighted fluid stencil and $A^{-1}$ at walls.
An additional MPI exchange supplies neighboring normals. Below
$|\vct g|=10^{-9}$, normal and capillary force are zero. The combined external
and capillary force enters both Guo collision and physical half-force
velocity; incoming initialization subtracts its capillary half-force before
the initial collision. Recoloring and the selected wall projection remain
common. This is a separate force-operator comparison, not a wall-only
ablation or replication of a published complete model.
The experimental force accepts closed VD/GV flow at equal reference
density and standard pressure weights. A guarded flat D2Q9 pressure
extension is assessed below: it requires ghost color, conservative phase
transfer and a pure force-free reservoir collar. Velocity reservoirs and
selective films remain unsupported. Neither option changes the legacy defaults.

An optional gradient form evaluates the same continuum force as
\begin{equation}
 \vct F_s=-\frac{\sigma}{2}\vct n
       [(I-\vct n\vct n):\nabla\vct g].
\end{equation}
It differentiates the unnormalized gradient with the same fluid stencil.
The identity follows from
$(I-\vct n\vct n):\nabla\vct g=|\vct g|(I-\vct n\vct n):\nabla\vct n$;
its discrete realization differs from normal differentiation.
Related Hessian forms and their discretization sensitivity are already
studied in phase-field LBM \cite{sugimoto2026}. This control introduces
neither an adjusted cutoff nor a fitted surface-tension coefficient.

\subsection{Pore-scale observables}
For $N$ total voxels, $N_f$ pore voxels, and $\varphi_0=\rho_0/\rho$,
$\varphi_1=1-\varphi_0$ in the fluid, the analysis defines
\begin{equation}
 S_k=\frac{1}{N_f}\sum_{fluid}\varphi_k,\qquad
 q_k=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{fluid}\varphi_k u_x,\qquad q=q_0+q_1.
\end{equation}
These are phase-fraction-weighted \emph{advective} Darcy fluxes of the common
mixture velocity. They do not include the component segregation momentum
introduced by recoloring. Component masses $\sum\rho_k$ are monitored
separately; in a weakly compressible closed domain, fixed mass does not make
the volume-fraction saturation exactly constant.

At unit reference density, the single-phase body-force estimate is
$K=\nu_{ref}q_{ref}/F_x$, and a phase normalization is
$k_{r,k}=\nu_kq_k/(F_xK)$. The latter is independently verified only for the
isolated pure-phase pore bundle below. The SCSP output $\vct u_{ref}$ uses
reference-density momentum and equilibrium, whereas color flow divides
momentum by local total density. A pure-phase comparison consequently uses
$\vct u_{ref}/\rho$ at unit reference density. The rock comparison retains
this convention and its small finite-density differences explicitly.

\section{Physical origin of finite-interface transport bias}
\subsection{Hydraulic response of a diffuse viscosity profile}
Consider two equal-density fluids layered across a no-slip slit of width $H$,
driven parallel to their interface by uniform force $F$. In steady low-speed
flow, $d(\mu du/dy)/dy=-F$. Define the inverse-viscosity moments
\begin{equation}
 I_n=\int_0^H\frac{y^n}{\mu(y)}\,dy,\qquad n=0,1,2.
\end{equation}
Integrating the momentum balance and imposing $u(0)=u(H)=0$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \mu u'=F(I_1/I_0-y),\qquad
 Q=\int_0^H u\,dy=F\left(I_2-\frac{I_1^2}{I_0}\right).
 \label{eq:conductance}
\end{equation}
This elementary continuum relation evaluates the response of a specified
constitutive profile without solving the lattice dynamics. A sharp step,
the viscosity reconstructed from measured phase fractions, and a separately
calibrated tanh profile therefore provide distinct references. At unit
equal reference density, dynamic and kinematic viscosities coincide to
leading order; small density departures remain recorded in the data.

Let $s=y-H/2$, $\varphi=(1-\tanh(s/\xi))/2$, and
$r=\mu_1/\mu_0$. For a well-separated interface, the integrated excess shear
compliance relative to the sharp step is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_A=\frac{\xi}{2}\left(\frac1{\mu_1}-\frac1{\mu_0}\right)\ln r,
 \qquad \mathcal C_H=0.
\end{equation}
The subscripts denote arithmetic and harmonic interpolation. Harmonic
interpolation cancels this zeroth-moment excess, but the stress varies across
the interface in pressure-driven flow. Its first and second weighted moments
still change the conductance. Writing $\gamma=(r-1)/(r+1)$, expansion of
Eq.~\ref{eq:conductance} about the centered sharp interface gives
\begin{align}
 \frac{Q_A-Q_{sharp}}{Q_{sharp}}&=
 -\frac{3\gamma^3\ln r}{4-3\gamma^2}\frac{\xi}{H}
 +O((\xi/H)^2),\\
 \frac{Q_H-Q_{sharp}}{Q_{sharp}}&=
 \frac{2\pi^2\gamma^2}{4-3\gamma^2}\left(\frac{\xi}{H}\right)^2
 +O((\xi/H)^4).
 \label{eq:thininterface}
\end{align}
Exponential wall-tail corrections vanish in the separated-interface limit.
The supplement derives these expressions. They describe the stated
one-dimensional constitutive problem, not universal error laws for curved
interfaces or heterogeneous rock. Harmonic interpolation's improved leading
order therefore does not imply superior accuracy at every confinement.

Three independent unforced planar calculations calibrate $W_{0.9}$ at
$\beta=0.35,0.7,0.9$. The widths are 9.376, 4.765 and 3.929 nodes;
$\xi=W_{0.9}/[2\operatorname{atanh}(0.9)]$ specifies a tanh approximation
without fitting any velocity. Eighteen forced calculations combine these
parameters with $H=16,32,64$, both closures and $(\tau_0,\tau_1)=(0.6,1.4)$.
Force scales as $H^{-3}$ and surface tension as $H^{-1}$, with fixed
relaxation times. Fixed $\beta$ makes the physical interface shrink during
this diffusive-scale sequence, so it is not a fixed-interface spatial
convergence test. All late relative velocity changes are below $10^{-11}$.

At $H=16$, $\beta=0.35$, arithmetic interpolation decreases the sampled
volume flux by 9.19\%, while harmonic interpolation increases it by 21.59\%.
Independent planar-tanh predictions give $-9.08$\% and $+21.30$\%.
The corresponding profile discrepancies against the \emph{sharp} solution
are 17.02\% and 24.66\%, but discrepancies against the continuum solution
using linear interpolation of the measured diffuse profile are 0.160\% and
0.100\%. Shape-preserving cubic interpolation gives 0.121\% and 0.273\%.
Most of the apparent failure is consequently the response of the chosen
finite constitutive transition, rather than failure of the solver to recover
its continuum momentum balance.

Fig.~\ref{fig:transport} also shows the competing effect of sharpening an
interface. At $H=16$, increasing $\beta$ from 0.35 to 0.9 reduces the
arithmetic sharp-flux bias, but increases its measured-diffuse profile error
from 0.160\% to 0.753\% with the specified linear reconstruction; the
corresponding shape-preserving cubic values are 0.121\% and 0.443\%.
At $H=64$, $\beta=0.9$, the harmonic discrepancy is 0.0117\% for linear
interpolation and 0.0482\% for the cubic reference. Quadrature refinement
changes a reference profile by at most $9.12\times10^{-8}$ relative to the
measured velocity, whereas interpolation changes can be much larger.
These small residuals include reference-reconstruction sensitivity and
finite-density departures; they are not isolated lattice truncation errors.
Interface confinement and lattice representation
must therefore be assessed separately. The remaining error is not assigned
exclusively to one discrete operator, and these flat interfaces do not test
curved-interface spurious currents or capillary trapping.

\subsection{Wall direction and capillary-stress magnitude}
The capillary second moment is proportional to
$|\vct g|(\vct n\vct n-I)$, so setting $\vct n$ alone does not set the stress
magnitude. On a flat D2Q9 lower wall, a locally linear gradient has the
clipped-stencil transformation
\begin{equation}
 \vct g_{clip}=\operatorname{diag}(5/6,1/2)\vct g,\qquad
 \frac{|\vct g_{clip}|}{|\vct g|}
 =\sqrt{\frac{25}{36}\sin^2\theta+\frac14\cos^2\theta}.
 \label{eq:wallmagnitude}
\end{equation}
The recovered wall rotation preserves the magnitude of the gradient it
receives. Even after an imposed direction correction, the local perturbation
amplitude thus retains this linear-field attenuation: 0.764 at 60 or 120
degrees and 0.833 at 90 degrees. Moment reconstruction removes that specific
linear-stencil bias. It does not prove discrete mechanical equilibrium at a
nonlinear contact line or prescribe an accurate curved-wall normal. The
measured static-angle improvement and increased parasitic flow reported
below are consistent with needing both checks; Eq.~\ref{eq:wallmagnitude}
alone is not a quantitative prediction of the observed capillary pressure
or spurious velocity.

\section{Illustrative verification}
All runs use double precision, GCC 13.3, CMake 3.28.3 and Open MPI 4.1.6 on
Linux. Dedicated workstation timings are described separately below.
The tested physical range is low-speed flow at unit, equal reference density,
$\tau_k\in\{0.6,0.8,1,1.4\}$ and prescribed $\sigma=0.005$ for droplet cases.
The supplement supplies derivations, measurement protocols and run identities.

\subsection{Force, viscosity and restart}
In the original MRT operator, $S$ was fixed at the first-phase relaxation
time. Pure second-phase pressure-channel fields were therefore identical
when its relaxation time changed. The original velocity-shift force has
momentum increment $s_j\tau F$: D3Q19 conserved rates make it vanish, while
D2Q9's old conserved rates produce a relaxation-dependent force.
The same fixed relaxation also changes capillary stress: at unchanged
prescribed $\sigma=0.005$, swapping $(\tau_0,\tau_1)=(0.6,1.4)$ to
$(1.4,0.6)$ gives inferred original surface tensions 0.00315 and 0.00764.
Local relaxation restores the intended surface-tension scale.

Sixteen uniform-force cases spanning both models, lattices, collision
operators and two viscosities agree with $u(100)=10^{-4}$ within
$3.2\times10^{-16}$. Twenty-four force-driven channel cases use halfway walls
and compare with $u(y)=F_xy(H-y)/(2\mu)$. Force scales as $H^{-3}$ while
relaxation time is fixed, preserving diffusive physical scaling. SRT errors
fall by approximately four on each doubling from $H=8$ to 16 to 32.
MRT errors are about $1.4\times10^{-8}$ or smaller, consistent with the
remaining transient at the selected run duration (Fig.~\ref{fig:channel}).

Continuous and restarted uniform-force and three-dimensional droplet runs

one-to-two-rank restart. Invalid parameter cases exit with failure. Algebraic

below $9\times10^{-16}$.

For two equal-density layers at viscosity ratio 9:1, the arithmetic
interpolation has 5.37\% relative profile error at $H=64$ against the
sharp-interface solution. Its error against a continuum solution using the
measured diffuse viscosity profile is 0.22\%. Harmonic interpolation reduces
the sharp-interface discrepancy to 0.81\%, while the diffuse-reference error
is 0.23\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:layered}). Thus the major change is a constitutive
transition bias, not an improved collision formula.

\subsection{Interfaces and wetting}
Planar interfaces conserve component mass and have spurious speed below
$5\times10^{-16}$. The measured distance between $\chi=-0.9$ and $+0.9$ is
4.765 nodes for D2Q9 and 5.289 for D3Q19 at $\beta=0.7$; D2Q9 widths at
$\beta=0.35$ and 0.9 are 9.376 and 3.929 nodes respectively. Interface width
is measured rather than equated to the input parameter.

Pressure is averaged in bulk regions outside the diffuse interface, and
radius is obtained by orthogonal fitting of subgrid contour intersections.
At 12000 steps, D2Q9 Laplace errors for initial radii 12, 20 and 28 are
1.50\%, 0.50\% and 0.23\%. The D3Q19 errors for radii 8, 12 and 16 are
4.96\%, 1.92\% and 0.99\%; the largest case is continued to 8000 steps.
The smallest spheres contain few interface widths and quantify finite-width
bias, rather than establish a sharp-interface asymptotic regime
(Fig.~\ref{fig:laplace}). Unequal-relaxation cases also retain the prescribed
surface-tension scale; their detailed errors are tabulated in the supplement.

Sessile-drop measurements use the physical halfway wall $y=0.5$ and measure

\subsection{A discrete neutral-wall fixed point for cosine recoloring}
The zero-sigma result permits an independent phase-only experiment.
At unit total density and zero velocity, recoloring sets outgoing
phase populations directly from $\varphi$ and the projected unit normal.
For a one-dimensional profile along a Cartesian D2Q9 wall, denote coordinate
and diagonal weights by $a=1/9$ and $b=1/36$, and set
$J_j=s_j\varphi_j(1-\varphi_j)$, where $s_j$ is the sign of the active
central color derivative. The bulk scalar recurrence is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phaserecurrence}
 \varphi'_j-\varphi_j=A(\varphi_{j-1}-2\varphi_j+\varphi_{j+1})
                 +\beta B(J_{j-1}-J_{j+1}),\qquad
 A=a+2b,\quad B=a+\sqrt{2}b.
\end{equation}
Local bounce-back at a neutral wall instead gives
$A_w=a+b$ and $B_w=a+b/\sqrt{2}$. Matching the interface direction does
not match this segregation-to-diffusion ratio. The independent recurrence
reproduces the full zero-sigma solver profile to within
$8\times10^{-14}$ in phase fraction.

Multiply only the two wall-parallel coordinate recoloring terms by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wallbalance}
 k=\frac{(a+b)(a+\sqrt{2}b)/(a+2b)-b/\sqrt{2}}{a}
   =0.9511844635\ldots .
\end{equation}
Then $B_w^{new}=ak+b/\sqrt{2}$ and $B_w^{new}/A_w=B/A$.
For a lifted one-dimensional profile with a common active-gradient mask,
the wall update residual is $A_w/A=5/6$ times the bulk residual.
Thus a bulk discrete stationary profile remains stationary at the wall;
the transient wall operator still differs from the bulk operator.
Opposite links have the same multiplier, so component mass is conserved
locally, and complementary recoloring preserves every total population.
This lattice-weight relation contains no calibrated parameter.
It concerns the recovered cosine normalization. Projection recoloring
specified in published work already gives the stationary wall/bulk ratio;
the matched comparison below bounds the significance of this correction.

The common mask is substantive. At a tangent-profile extremum, the clipped
nonlinear stencil can produce a false normal derivative even when the
tangent derivative vanishes. A 65-cell manufactured control failed without
addressing this defect. The optional rule uses the reconstructed tangent
magnitude at a neutral wall, discarding the raw normal component before
applying the unchanged $10^{-9}$ cutoff and angle projection.
The tested fixed-point extension supports flat Cartesian D2Q9 walls and
equal reference densities with standard weights. Corners, staircase walls,
open reservoirs and selective films are rejected; a nonneutral angle is
an exploratory use outside the derivation.

\subsection{Temporal aliasing and the limits of the correction}
From a step initialization, the corrected CSF slit still develops a small
raw velocity. Adjacent fields reveal sign reversal rather than a stationary
circulation (Fig.~\ref{fig:wallbalance}). For the H16, $\beta=0.7$ normal
curvature case, the raw maximum speed is \WallRawSpeed, while its
adjacent-step average is below \WallPairSpeedBound. The tangential staggered
momentum projection accounts for more than \WallStaggeredEnergyPercent\%
of that component's energy. Raw fields are retained; the average is a
diagnostic and is not substituted for a solution.
Across the three slit widths, its raw $\mu U_{max}/\sigma$ decreases from
about $1.93\times10^{-6}$ to $2.14\times10^{-7}$ under the specified joint
scaling, rather than retaining the uncorrected neutral value near
$3.3\times10^{-4}$. This observation does not remove the period-two state
or establish convergence at fixed physical interface width.

This mode is established in LBM dispersion analysis \cite{lallemand2000}
and related two-phase work \cite{guo2011}. In the linearized system,
$\delta f_i=3w_i e_{ix}A(-1)^x$ is an equilibrium momentum perturbation.
Collision preserves it, whereas streaming, including wall-parallel
bounce-back, changes its sign. Its eigenvalue is $-1$ rather than a
viscous decay factor. Manufactured nonlinear equilibrium seeds also
propagate with period two in both SRT and MRT; switching between these
operators is therefore not a damping remedy. The previous even-report
stopping test accepted such a state. The adjacent-step criterion rejects
it while retaining termination of stationary controls.

The uncorrected CSF slit retains substantial velocity even after the
adjacent-step average; its prior residual is not explained away by this
mode. The gradient curvature form is likewise not uniformly superior:
it lowers the corrected neutral raw velocity but increases bulk D2Q9
Laplace discrepancies to approximately 1.78\% and 0.385\% at radii 12 and 24,
compared with 1.39\% and 0.303\% for normal differentiation.
Exploratory 60/120-degree controls also retain larger pressure bias and
slower phase relaxation with the wall multiplier. After four initial
durations, normal-CSF 60-degree pressure discrepancies are 9.34\%, 5.59\%
and 4.37\% at $H=16,32,48$, with final color RMS changes below
$10^{-9}$ and mass drift below $10^{-11}$. The corresponding uncorrected reconstructed-CSF discrepancies
are 7.22\%, 3.12\% and 1.71\% after their documented longer holds.
Those negative controls
exclude treating a neutral fixed-point derivation as a generally validated
wetting model. Full histories and longer holds are retained in
\texttt{wall-consistency.json}; no entry pressure or trapping law is inferred.

\subsection{Wall transport and physical contact-angle location}
Matching a stationary segregation ratio does not match transient transport.
For two adjacent wall fluid nodes $L,R$, let $p,m$ denote the outgoing
diagonals directed into the solid with opposite tangent components.
Writing $f_T=f_0+f_1$ and $\varphi=\sum_i f_{0i}/\sum_i f_{Ti}$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wallphaseflux}
 J_{LR}=f_{0p}(L)-f_{0m}(R)
       -\frac{\varphi_L+\varphi_R}{2}[f_{Tp}(L)-f_{Tm}(R)].
\end{equation}
Add $J_{LR}$ to the corresponding incoming diagonal at $R$ and subtract
it from its partner at $L$ after local bounce-back. The second phase is
complementary to each original bounce-back total. Face transfers conserve
each component globally and leave total-fluid reflection unchanged.
Pure phases give $J_{LR}=0$, including nonuniform total populations.
The face fraction is an explicit numerical closure, not a thermodynamic
or general moving-contact-line law.

At unit density and rest, the total-population difference vanishes and
the rule reduces to established nonlocal specular-forward scalar transport
\cite{ginzburg2015}. The additional neutral phase update is
$b\Delta_x\varphi+\beta b(J_{j-1}-J_{j+1})/\sqrt{2}$, restoring both
$A$ and $B$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:phaserecurrence}. Independently generated
scalar trajectories match the bulk recurrence throughout relaxation;
six full-solver early trajectories match it within \FluxTransientBound.
Nonzero-sigma neutral runs retain unfiltered maximum speed below
\FluxNeutralSpeedBound, including SRT/MRT and odd periodic lengths.
This avoids excitation of the observed plane-control oscillation; the
undamped lattice eigenmode still exists and no damping was added.

Nonneutral pressure requires a separate location control. The original
rotation specifies the normal at the first fluid center, whereas the
physical wall lies half a lattice spacing away. For a sharp circular
meniscus, imposing the same angle on the first/last centers can replace
the physical spacing $H$ by $H-1$, giving the geometric estimate
$1/(H-1)$ for relative pressure bias. This is a mechanism to test, not
a fitted effective width. A halfway control instead sets the first-row
inward-normal cosine to $c_1=(2\cos\theta+c_2)/3$, so
$1.5c_1-0.5c_2=\cos\theta$, and preserves the central tangent derivative.

A second control uses the published scalar ghost continuation
\cite{liu2015}, with the sign converted to this code's angle convention:
$\chi_g=\chi_1-\cot\theta|1.5g_{t1}-0.5g_{t2}|$.
The full color stencil then includes the virtual values and receives no
subsequent center-angle rotation. This reproduces that ghost formula under
LBFlow's remaining operators; its segregation weights and MRT spectrum
are not the complete published model. An independent outgoing-state
gradient calculation verifies the implementation.

All three-width 60-degree comparisons in Fig.~\ref{fig:wallflux} use four
initial durations, fixed $\beta=0.7$, common input surface tension and the
same physical wall spacing. With ordinary rotation, phase transfer alone
worsens pressure. With ghost reconstruction, transferred-phase discrepancies
are \FluxGhostPressureSixteen\%, \FluxGhostPressureThirtyTwo\% and
\FluxGhostPressureFortyEight\% at $H=16,32,48$. Yet nonneutral currents remain,
and the H16 controls (seven angles at $\beta=0.7$, three beta values at
60/90/120 degrees) do not show uniform improvement
in pressure, shape and velocity. A small H16 halfway/bounce-back pressure
error also fails to remain small under refinement; it is not a general
accuracy result. Circle-exclusion and bulk-sampling sensitivity are retained.
For ghost/transfer, the H16 circle-extrapolated angles span 57.49--59.14
degrees over the specified exclusions, versus 59.60--59.61 degrees at H48;
they are not direct contact-angle measurements at the wall.
Adjacent fields still show nonneutral two-step oscillations: in the H16
ghost/transfer control, one-step maximum color change is approximately
$6.65\times10^{-7}$ despite much smaller even-time differences.
The tested pressure-coefficient change over adjacent steps remains below
$6\times10^{-9}$. Stable pressure therefore does not establish a stationary
phase/velocity field, and temporal averaging does not eliminate the
nonneutral circulation. Relative component/total mass drift over the
recorded trajectories remains below $10^{-11}$.
These controls distinguish transport and location defects without
establishing dynamic or curved-wall wetting accuracy.

\subsection{Published core controls at matched phase transport}
The ghost-color comparison initially retains the recovered recoloring and
MRT definitions. To separate these from wall treatment, two independent
controls reproduce the explicitly specified core operators of
Liu et al. \cite{liu2015}. Projection recoloring uses
\begin{equation}
 R_i=\beta_p\rho\varphi(1-\varphi)w_i(\vct e_i\cdot\vct n),
\end{equation}
instead of dividing this projection by $|\vct e_i|$.
The published D2Q9 raw-moment spectrum, in the existing row order, is
$(1,1.63,1.14,1,1.92,1,1.92,1/\tau,1/\tau)$.
The matching Guo source and physical half-force velocity preserve the
momentum increment even when its relaxation rate is one.
Only CSF is admitted with this spectrum: its energy rates do not justify
reusing the legacy perturbation-tension mapping. The fluid-only
normal derivative at walls remains an explicit LBFlow choice; these
controls are not a full replication of an incompletely specified scheme.

For an axial rest profile, projection segregation has $B_p=A=a+2b$,
and local bounce-back has $B_{p,w}=A_w=a+b$. It already preserves the
bulk stationary ratio without the cosine-derived multiplier.
To compare transport independently of pressure, set
\begin{equation}
 \beta_p=\beta_c\frac{a+\sqrt{2}b}{a+2b}
             =\CoreMatchedBeta\quad(\beta_c=0.7).
\end{equation}
This matches the complete axial bulk recurrence, not just a fitted width.
Independent 128-cell planar profiles agree within
$\CoreMatchedProfileError$; both have $W_{0.9}=4.765235$.
Projection with the unadjusted $\beta_p=0.7$ instead has width 4.450814.
The cosine weights $w_i/|\vct e_i|$ have a fourth-order isotropy defect
$T_{xxxx}-3T_{xxyy}=0.0650874$, whereas standard projection weights
give zero to roundoff. Equal axial recurrence therefore does not make
the multidimensional discrete operators identical. The supplied oblique
width diagnostic also retains its different sampling interval and
interpolation bias; its full difference is not attributed to this tensor.

With ghost color and phase transfer, the matched projection/legacy-MRT
pressure discrepancies are $+0.517$, $-0.336$ and $-0.702$\% at
$H=16,32,48$ after four initial durations. The published-MRT values are
$+0.551$, $-0.341$ and $-0.716$\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:core}).
The sign reversal and larger final discrepancy preclude using the close
H16 result as uniform accuracy evidence. Raw nonneutral speeds decrease,
but adjacent color still differs from its two-step state. At H16 the
one-step change is approximately $6.66\times10^{-7}$, compared with
$3.47\times10^{-12}$ over two steps. Projection and a different kinetic
spectrum do not eliminate the conserved-momentum lattice mode.

Its force coupling can be checked exactly. On an even periodic axis, or
the reflecting wall-normal axis, let $P_t$ be the fluid-node mean of
physical momentum times the alternating spatial sign, and $G_t$ the same
projection of force density. Outgoing momentum is $P_t+G_t/2$; streaming
or reflection changes its sign. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parityforce}
 P_{t+1}=-P_t+\frac{G_{t+1}-G_t}{2}.
\end{equation}
Physical fields, native outgoing populations and the actual force audit
satisfy this relation within $5.1\times10^{-19}$ in the original held
nonneutral control. Explicitly setting $\sigma=0$ makes circulation
decay while alternating momentum remains; this diagnostic changes the
physics and does not filter production velocities. Odd-periodic-domain
and exact-translation controls are distinguished in the supplement.

The MRT change also affects wall hydraulics. In a pure low-speed slit,
define $\Lambda=(\tau-1/2)(1/s_q-1/2)$ and
$C=4\Lambda/3-1/4$. The midpoint profile is
\begin{equation}
 u(y)=\frac{F}{2\nu}[y(H-y)+C],\qquad y=j+\tfrac12.
\end{equation}
Legacy rates give $\Lambda=3/16$ and $C=0$;
the published $s_q=1.92$ gives $\Lambda=(\tau-1/2)/48$.
Eighteen pure-channel controls match this independent reference within
relative profile error $\CoreMaxHydraulicError$.
The largest unshifted flux discrepancy is approximately $-2.30$\% at
$H=8$, $\tau=0.6$. A lower static current cannot be interpreted as
unchanged dynamic wall response. Bulk droplet controls at matched
transport likewise retain their measured pressure and residual velocity.

\subsection{Guarded pressure-driven fronts}
A pressure boundary introduces component mass, so closed-mass conservation
alone cannot validate it. The new experiment admits two opposed axial PB
planes only with flat ghost-color walls and phase transfer. At every
incoming/outgoing state, each prescribed phase must remain pure within
$10^{-9}$ on its PB plane and two interior strips. The actual first-strip
gradient must be below the existing $10^{-9}$ cutoff, keeping the
uncorrected Zou--He reconstruction force-free. The solver fails when the
interface reaches this collar. Phase-only wall transfers on faces touching
PB nodes are disabled, including the wrap between opposite reservoirs.
Velocity reservoirs, films and irregular walls are outside this extension.

For interior VD/GV nodes, independently integrate every outgoing kinetic
population entering from or leaving for PB. Solid links return by reflection
and are excluded. The exact accounting is
\begin{equation}
 M_k(t)-M_k(t_0)=\sum_{n=t_0}^{t-1}
   [\mathcal J_{k,\mathrm{PB}\to\mathrm{int}}(n)
    -\mathcal J_{k,\mathrm{int}\to\mathrm{PB}}(n)]+\epsilon_k.
\end{equation}
The boundary-node sum of $\rho u$ is not substituted for these link fluxes.
Restart establishes a new accounting baseline while retaining the same
outgoing numerical state. Two-phase fields and populations are bitwise
identical across the tested rank/restart changes. Across the pressure
campaign, the largest budget residual relative to initial interior mass
is $\CoreMaxPressureBudget$.

Pure-channel pressure controls use both flow directions, both phases and
both spectra, at $H=8,16,32$ and $\tau=1,1.55$. The independently measured
central pressure gradient and specified midpoint offset reproduce the
central profiles within $1.92\times10^{-5}$. The central gradient differs
slightly from the nominal endpoint pressure divided by length, so measured
pure-channel conductance is retained separately.

a $4H$ slit. Remote corridors explicitly combine donor phase populations
into the prescribed pure phase, preserving each total population and its
momentum. No damping or surface-tension fit is applied. Equal viscosities,
60-degree walls and independently matched beta are used at H16/H32;
$\sigma$ and duration scale as in the closed controls.
The volume-equivalent front coordinate is
$X_V=1/2+H^{-1}\sum_{\mathrm{int}}\varphi$; centerline and first-fluid-row
crossings are retained as separate diagnostics.

Reservoir differences $0.98,1,1.02$ times
$p_Y=2\sigma\cos\theta/H$ give both motion directions. Increasing the
duration fourfold to 32000/128000 steps changes the three-point linear
zero-drift pressure ratio by at most $\CoreMaxDurationRatioChange$.
Additional pressure pairs, prespecified at the separately measured closed
pressure plus/minus $0.00025p_Y$, still give opposite drift signs at
both durations. The linear zero-drift estimate differs from the closed
pressure by at most \CoreMaxZeroClosedPercent\% of $p_Y$

consistency check, not a geometric pore-throat trapping threshold.

The measured slope of front speed against pressure remains approximately
11.3--17.8\% lower than pure-channel conductance. A zero-sigma diagnostic at unchanged reservoir

regularization are established physical issues \cite{huh1971,akai2018};
these comparisons do not isolate a physical slip length, a constitutive
contact-line law or a general numerical-error mechanism. Static pressure
agreement alone is insufficient evidence for quantitative displacement
rates in digital-rock pores.

\subsection{Analytic pore flux and capillary entry}
Two parallel, isolated slit pores have widths $H_0=8s$ and $H_1=16s$,
with total specimen height $36s$, for $s=1,2,3$. Each pore contains a pure
component; separate solid walls prevent a fluid-fluid interface. Periodic
flow is driven by $F_x=10^{-6}s^{-3}$ with both permutations of
$(\tau_0,\tau_1)=(0.6,1.4)$. The independently sampled Poiseuille reference is
\begin{equation}
 q_k=\frac{F_x}{\nu_k(36s)}\left(\frac{H_k^3}{12}+\frac{H_k}{24}\right).
\end{equation}
The second term accounts for midpoint sampling of the analytic velocity.
Single-phase runs on the same geometry supply $K$ for normalization. Across
both D2Q9 and extruded D3Q19, the maximum phase-flux discrepancy is
$\BundleFluxError$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pores}), consistent with the remaining
transient at the selected duration and roundoff. This verifies phase attribution and
viscosity normalization in a controlled pore system; it is not evidence for
general relative-permeability curves in connected rock pores.

Pressure boundaries then drive phase 0 into an initially phase-1-filled
60-degree slit. Its sharp static reference is
$p_c=2\sigma\cos\theta/H$. At pressure drops $0.5p_c$ and $1.5p_c$, the
late interface respectively retreats and advances. Diffusive refinement uses
$H=16s$, $\sigma=0.005/s$ and duration $8000s^2$, for $s=1,2,4$.
Four D3Q19 square-tube controls at $H=16$ use the force-balance reference
$p_c=4\sigma\cos\theta/H$. Both gradient variants give the expected late
direction in these controls. In the square tube, however, inferring an
entry pressure from a developed-flow hydraulic resistance gives deviations
of approximately 17--25\% for the clipped stencil and 9--16\% for
renormalization. Contact-line dissipation, voxel corners and diffuse wetting
are not removed by this diagnostic. The result checks the direction of
displacement and quantifies limitations; it does not calibrate a static
capillary-pressure curve or a dynamic contact-angle law.

\subsection{Public Bentheimer specimen}
The segmented 4.54~$\mu$m Bentheimer image of Shah, Crawshaw and Boek
\cite{shah2014} supplies a public, CC BY 4.0 input. A centered
$48^3$ crop starts at $(263,263,263)$ in the original $575^3$ array.
Its porosity is $\RockPorosity$, and all pores belong to one connected
component after periodic face connections. No pore removal, filtering or
segmentation change is performed. Phase 0 initially occupies pores whose
periodic distance to the grain-center mask is at least three original
voxels; phase 1 occupies the remaining pore space. This deterministic
morphological rule specifies an initial state and does not prescribe an
experimental drainage history.

Single-phase MRT flow at $\tau=1$ yields
$K=\RockK\ \mathrm{m}^2$. Halving and doubling the force give a relative
spread of $\RockForceSpread$. Independent LBPM v2026.04 calculations first
pass three analytic slit controls, then use the same exact periodically
padded mask, force, viscosity and averaging volume. Across five matched
rock cases, the largest permeability difference is
\RockReferenceDifference\ percent. Both solvers retain small finite-density
convention differences; converted library permeability columns are not used.
Pure-phase color-flow reductions agree with density-normalized SCSP velocity
within $10^{-11}$ in the tested cases. These are numerical crosschecks on
the specified periodically repeated specimen, not a comparison with
laboratory sandstone permeability.

Each original voxel is also divided into eight voxels, preserving the
binary box geometry and initial phase volumes. Force, surface tension and
duration are scaled diffusively. The resulting single-phase permeability
decreases by \RefinedKChange\ percent. The independent refined LBPM reference
reproduces this shift. This is a discretization sensitivity
on a fixed segmented specimen; two resolutions do not establish an
asymptotic convergence rate, and subdivision adds no imaging information.

Closed two-phase runs at prescribed angles 60 and 120 degrees retain
component masses to a maximum relative drift of $\RockMassDrift$.
The zero-force control also evolves, so its motion cannot be attributed to
the imposed body force. Physical interface relaxation and possible numerical
currents have not been separated in this geometry. In the forced 60-degree case, the color

\subsection{Workstation performance}
This timing campaign uses the frozen perturbation executable recorded before
the experimental capillary extension. It does not measure the new CSF branch
or establish the runtime of the revised build. The complete executable hash
remains in the timing data.
A separate $96^3$ crop provides a fixed two-phase timing workload with
\PerfFluidNodes\ fluid nodes. The actual D3Q19/MRT numerical path is timed
for \PerfSteps\ updates at one, two, four and eight MPI ranks on an
Intel Core Ultra 9 285K workstation. One full warm-up per rank count is
discarded; three sequential measured repetitions interleave rank counts.

\section{Impact and limitations}
The physical analysis distinguishes two questions that can otherwise be
confounded: whether a solver recovers the momentum balance for its chosen
diffuse viscosity profile, and whether that profile represents the desired
sharp-interface flow. In the controlled slit, the independent continuum
reference accounts for most of the transport discrepancy. Neither good
conservation nor selection of a familiar viscosity closure removes it.
The local wall analysis makes the analogous distinction between an imposed
interface direction and capillary-stress magnitude.
Direct closed-slit pressure and zero-sigma controls further show why
surface-force, wetting and phase-boundary consistency must be considered
together. The experimental CSF branch improves residual flow in its measured
scope but retains pressure bias and wall-color distortion before the
phase-boundary correction. The cosine-specific neutral fixed point removes that
phase defect in its tested geometry, while revealing period-two velocity
and failing to establish nonneutral accuracy. Complete-transient phase
reflection avoids that plane-control excitation; separating the contact-angle
location and independently matched published core controls further improve
measured slit pressure, while nonneutral
currents and contour sensitivity remain. This established
force formulation is not evidence for a new bulk theory.

The guarded flat-slit pressure experiments connect closed capillary pressure
to bidirectional front drift, while revealing a separate mobility difference.
Extending these findings to capillary trapping requires a different test.
Quasistatic invasion and retraction in a specified pore throat must resolve
the pressure interval, contact-line rate dependence, interface width and
wall representation before a change in occupancy is called physical
pinning or hysteresis. Such thresholds are not measured by either the earlier
direction checks or the new uniform-slit drift brackets. The controlled viscous
asymptotics also cannot be assigned directly to a curved interface in rock.
The accompanying code, exact inputs and checks support that next physical
study; current external adoption or cluster performance is not inferred
from source history.

The validation covers equal density, standard pressure weights and the
reported viscosity range. Higher density contrast, general moving contact-line laws,
curved three-dimensional wetting accuracy, material-junction pinning and thermodynamic
consistency remain unvalidated. Enhanced equilibria and central-moment
developments address wider-density and Galilean-invariance issues
\cite{lafarge2021,saito2023}. Those results cannot be transferred to this
recovered equilibrium without additional changes and tests. Pressure-derived
legacy permeability diagnostics use a reference viscosity and are not used
as validated phase-specific permeabilities in the rock application. The
pore-bundle normalization is verified only in its isolated pure-phase geometry. Restart histories
reset conservatively and the native population format is not claimed to be
portable across unlike machine representations.

\section{Conclusions}
Finite interface thickness changes transport through the constitutive
profile as well as through its lattice representation. In a centered
equal-density layered slit, inverse-viscosity moments explain the distinct
leading arithmetic and harmonic flux shifts. Harmonic interpolation removes
the leading excess shear compliance, while a thicker transition can still
increase pressure-driven flow substantially. Sharpening an interface reduces
this constitutive bias but can increase residual profile error. The
eighteen-case study distinguishes these mechanisms with independently
calibrated widths and a continuum momentum reference.

At a wall, rotating a clipped gradient to the desired direction preserves
its attenuated magnitude. Linear moment reconstruction addresses that
specific issue; measured contact-angle improvement does not establish
uniformly improved parasitic flow or dynamic capillary response. The
cosine-normalized phase recurrence supplies a parameter-free fixed-point
correction; published projection recoloring already preserves that
stationary ratio. Exact axial transport matching permits a controlled
multidimensional comparison. Adjacent-step tests keep undamped lattice modes separate from
stationary flow. Conservative phase transfer additionally restores the
complete neutral recurrence. A physical-wall location and published
ghost-color comparison show that this transport condition alone does not
establish nonneutral pressure accuracy. The guarded pressure experiment
connects zero drift to the measured closed pressure while separating front
mobility from pure-channel conductance. None supplies a general contact-line law. The
public-rock application adds an independently checked geometric flow
baseline and exposes inadequate interface separation and continued
two-phase evolution. It supports neither a steady relative-permeability
curve nor a quantified trapping threshold.

LBFlow provides reproducible implementations and measurements for this
physical analysis, including corrected local relaxation and forcing,
mass monitoring and consistent MPI restart. These results motivate a
controlled pore-throat study of capillary transport with separate
confinement, wall and rate checks. A new bulk multiphase theory or universal
wetting method is not claimed.
