\title{Predicting numerical capillary resistance from discrete interface kinetics in LBFlow}
\author{Yu Zhou\corref{cor1}}
\address{Independent Researcher, Canada}
\ead{[private contact omitted]}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\begin{abstract}
A numerical interface can change capillary transport even when its width,
leading diffusion and hydraulic wall slip are matched. We quantify this
effect in LBFlow, a C++/MPI color-gradient lattice Boltzmann program.
An exact zero-flow phase spectrum, including unit-normal variation, predicts
decay rates in \MajorWaveCount\ complete simulations within \MajorWavePercent\%,
with three transverse modes and a separate width control.
A formal isolated-interface expansion identifies the numerical coefficient
$K_{\mathrm{num}}=Dh^2/4$ at the tested $D=1/6$.
Adding only the corresponding transverse fourth-order term to an independent
Stokes/finite-volume reference reduces three neutral-slit mean-speed gaps
from 12.06--14.62\% to 0.275--1.023\%, using LBM as the common
denominator, without fitting the coefficient.
A low-force response reduction predicts the reference mean speeds within
\MajorReducedAllPercent\% across the tested grids, hydraulic slips and
surface tensions. Its left-null-vector mobility explains why numerical
shape relaxation can introduce a surface-tension dependence absent from
the untreated reduced response. Conservative wall/bulk interventions
localize the sensitivity to the prescribed wall neighborhood.
Continuous-source coupled manufactured tests recover near-second-order
phase and velocity convergence; full-field force audits bound the hydraulic
comparison separately. The resulting evidence identifies a numerical
kinetics/resistance mechanism in neutral equal-viscosity flow, rather than
a new bulk phase model or validated molecular contact-line law.
Digital-rock controls supply application context, with transient two-phase
rock motion explicitly outside the quantitative validation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Physical question and relation to prior work}
In pore-scale two-phase flow, the interface transmits capillary stress while
its motion changes the fluid configuration and hence the hydraulic resistance.
A diffuse interface also carries a computational regularization. Matching
its measured thickness does not necessarily match the kinetics of changing
its shape. This distinction matters particularly when a numerical interface
occupies a substantial fraction of a microscopic pore.

We ask whether a specified higher-order phase relaxation can explain a
measured capillary-resistance difference, and whether that difference can be
predicted without fitting a front velocity or an effective slip. The test
uses a neutral periodic slit with independently prescribed hydraulic slip,
equal fluid viscosities and small body force. This removes viscosity contrast,
nonneutral angle fitting and pressure-reservoir placement from the primary
comparison. It remains a controlled numerical contact-line setting rather
than a complete physical wetting model.

The underlying bulk model is established. Color-gradient perturbation and
recoloring have long-standing formulations
\cite{latva2005,reis2007,liu2012,liuerratum2012}.
Subhedar et al. derive the conservative Allen--Cahn description and the
anisotropy associated with cosine link normalization \cite{subhedar2020}.
Independent mobility and density-contrast extensions are also prior work
\cite{subhedar2022}. Haghani et al. develop a color-gradient-based phase-field
formulation separating interface capture from hydrodynamics and addressing
fluid invariance at density contrast \cite{haghani2024}.
Profile-preserving conservative transport and signed-distance approaches
likewise predate this study \cite{mirjalili2020,hao2024}.
Consequently the Allen--Cahn mapping, adjustable mobility, normal-profile
preservation and separation of interface and fluid equations are not claimed
as contributions of LBFlow.

Numerical wall transport and slip also have analytical and algorithmic
precedents \cite{ginzburg2015,ahmed2009}. Published color-conserving wetting
closures and force-consistent flat-wall treatments already address important
parts of this problem \cite{hollis2011,liu2015,yu2018}.
Physical diffuse-contact-line studies distinguish interface width from
an emergent slip length \cite{kusumaatmaja2016}; chemical-potential mobility
scalings from those studies cannot be assigned directly to this recoloring
map. Similarly, continuously equivalent surface-force formulas can have
different discrete behavior \cite{sugimoto2026}. A force rewrite alone
therefore does not establish a new model.

The contribution here is narrower and quantitatively testable. We
differentiate the complete finite-grid phase map, verify its decay rates
against the full solver, and derive a conditional fourth-order shape term.
We then intervene with that term alone, preserving the leading phase
equation and fluid solver. A separate low-force response calculation predicts
the resistance change and its dependence on surface tension; it does not
use the measured front data. Full-field hydraulic audits and coupled
manufactured tests make the accuracy and scope of the reference explicit.

The accompanying LBFlow program makes these operators, failed controls and
reproduction data inspectable. 
The supplement supplies viscosity, wetting, pressure-front,
digital-rock and older-kernel timing evidence. Those supporting studies
are distinct from the principal kinetics/resistance claim.

\section{Controlled numerical setting and program}
\subsection{Fluid and phase operators}
LBFlow supports D2Q9/D3Q19 streaming, SRT or raw-moment MRT, color-gradient
recoloring, voxel boundaries and MPI exchange. The primary experiment uses
D2Q9, equal unit reference densities, standard pressure weights,
$\tau_0=\tau_1=1$, legacy MRT, projection recoloring and curvature surface
forcing. These explicit choices exclude the historical cosine normalization
and unequal-density extensions from the main kinetic result.

With $\varphi=\rho_0/\rho$ and $\rho=\rho_0+\rho_1$, the force-corrected
velocity and pressure in lattice units are
\begin{equation}
 \rho\bm u_l=\sum_i\bm e_i(f_i^0+f_i^1)+\bm F_l/2,\qquad p_l=\rho/3.
\end{equation}
The combined body and surface force uses the established Guo source
\cite{guo2002}. The curvature force is
\begin{equation}
 \bm F_s=\sigma\kappa\nabla\varphi,\qquad
 \kappa=-(I-\bm n\bm n):\nabla\bm n,\qquad
 \bm n=\nabla\varphi/|\nabla\varphi|.
\end{equation}
The production derivative is the weighted lattice stencil, with fluid
reconstruction at walls and color-gradient cutoff $10^{-9}$.
The independent reference uses staggered faces and a logistic-distance
geometry; its field differences are measured rather than assumed absent.

Projection recoloring partitions the post-collision total population as
\begin{equation}
 f_i^{0,*}=\varphi f_i^{post}
 +\beta\rho\varphi(1-\varphi)w_i(\bm e_i\cdot\bm n_h),
 \qquad f_i^{1,*}=f_i^{post}-f_i^{0,*}.
\end{equation}
At constant-density rest this becomes
$w_i[\varphi+\beta\varphi(1-\varphi)\bm e_i\cdot\bm n_h]$.
Streaming these populations defines the nonlinear zero-flow phase map
$P(\varphi)$. The component corrections have opposite momentum and preserve
the total population. The full map, rather than only its leading equation,
is the object of the spectral calculation.

\subsection{Geometry, scaling and independently specified slip}
The physical slit has height $H_p=16$ and periodic length $4H_p=64$ in common
base units. We write $H$ for its wall-normal lattice-node count; the main
grids have $H=16,32,48$ and $h=H_p/H$. Diffusive scaling sets the physical
time step to $h^2$, with $\nu=D=1/6$, $\sigma_l=\sigma h$,
$F_l=Fh^3$ and $\lambda_l=\lambda/h$.
The corresponding velocity and pressure conversions are $\bm u=\bm u_l/h$
and $p=p_l/h^2$; pressure differences remove the arbitrary reference level.
The independently calibrated crossing width is
$W_{0.9}=4.765235$, so $W_{0.9}/H_p=0.297827$.
The LBM $\beta$ and reference logistic coefficient are determined from that
width, independently of speed. Their finite-grid values are not declared
identical; the supplement distinguishes sampled-width and fixed-continuum-
parameter reference refinement.

A phase bridge contains two spanning interfaces, with both translated by
the same transverse displacement. Neutral walls impose reflected scalar
transport. Hydraulic Navier slip is prescribed as $\lambda=1$, with
$\lambda=4$ as a separate control.
The bounce-back/specular fraction is
$s=\lambda_l/(\lambda_l+1/2)$; specular populations come from the forward
tangential neighbor. The associated conservative relative-phase transfer
is multiplied by $1-s$, avoiding a double transfer of the same scalar link.
Its rest-state reflection is independent of $s$.

Pure-channel runs verify the LBM midpoint profile
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slippure}
 u_j=\frac{F_l}{2\nu_l}
 [y_j(H-y_j)+\lambda_l H],\qquad y_j=j+\tfrac12.
\end{equation}
Eighteen signed controls at three slips, six further signed $\lambda=4$
controls, neutral equilibrium, rotation, MPI exchange and outgoing restart
checks are retained. The optional closure admits only the documented neutral,
equal-$\tau$ configurations. Slip is a numerical boundary control; it is
not inferred from a front velocity or identified with molecular slip.

For the independent Stokes/FV reference, the exact discrete pure profile is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fvpure}
 u_j=\frac{F}{2\nu}[y_j(H_p-y_j)+\lambda H_p+h^2/4],
 \qquad y_j=(j+\tfrac12)h.
\end{equation}
The $h^2/4$ difference between these discrete laws is retained.
Mean axial fluid speed $\overline u$, circular phase-centroid speed and
phase-volume interface displacement are separate observables.
The equivalent resistance is $R=F/\overline u$; comparisons of its excess
use each discretization's analytic pure-channel $R_0$.
Conservation, finite fields and actual output clocks are checked separately
from a small speed discrepancy.

\section{Accuracy and limits of the independent reference}
\subsection{Coupled continuous-source verification}
The independent calculation solves constant-viscosity Stokes flow using
Fourier modes and a streamfunction biharmonic inversion on a staggered mesh. Conservative
face advection and second-order Runge--Kutta advance the phase equation.
A logistic-distance face flux preserves its independently calibrated planar
profile. Existing component tests verify analytic no-slip/Navier hydraulics,
zero response to a discrete gradient force and prescribed phase translation.
Component tests alone do not verify the coupled calculation.

We therefore add a time-dependent manufactured paired interface, nonuniform
Navier flow and nonzero surface tension. External force and phase sources
are derived from continuous analytic derivatives, independently of the
implemented force, curvature and face flux. The length is 128, physical
height 16, $b=0.62$, $\nu=D=1/6$, $\sigma=.005$, and physical duration 16.
A smooth pure-tail join removes the bridge-distance cusp outside the
interface. Grids $H=16,32,64$ are tested at slips 1 and 4, plus an H32
half-step control; the construction and sources are in the supplement.

At slip 1 the final relative phase errors are
$2.6250\times10^{-6}$, $6.3202\times10^{-7}$ and $1.5685\times10^{-7}$,
with orders 2.054 and 2.011. Relative velocity errors are 0.026659,
0.006637 and 0.001656, with orders 2.006 and 2.002.
Slip 4 gives phase orders 2.030 and 2.006 and velocity orders 2.002 and
2.001 (Fig.~\ref{fig:majormanufactured}).
Maximum divergence is below $2.8\times10^{-20}$ and the absolute
source-integrated phase-mass residual is below $9.5\times10^{-13}$.
Small pure-tail overshoots, of order $8\times10^{-12}$, are retained.
They occur at the log-reconstruction regularization scale; the fields are
not clipped and no boundedness theorem is claimed.

The dynamic untreated reference is additionally refined to H64 and H96.
At matched sampled width its mean speeds are $1.16649\times10^{-5}$ and
$1.15173\times10^{-5}$, continuing the decreasing H16/H32/H48 sequence.
A separate fixed-$b$ sequence gives $1.18086\times10^{-5}$,
$1.16640\times10^{-5}$ and $1.15150\times10^{-5}$ at H48/H64/H96.
These tests do not establish a resolved continuum contact-line limit or
a uniform order for near-grid phase modes.
The faster optional sine-basis inversion solves the same Stokes matrix,
with full velocity differences below $4.2\times10^{-12}$ against the dense
inverse in nine random-force/slip/grid controls. It does not change the
spatial scheme or provide a performance claim for LBFlow.

\subsection{Full-field hydraulic and surface-force audit}
On two adjacent outgoing production states at each main grid, the solver's
own actual capillary force is recorded together with density and color.
These states agree with the saved physical fields within
$6.7\times10^{-16}$ in the audited observables.
The force is averaged onto MAC faces and solved independently.
Table~\ref{tab:majorfields} reports full relative $L_2$ differences after
the specified two-face MAC-to-cell velocity average.

At H48 the actual-force velocity discrepancy is 0.0928\%;
the same-phase reference-force discrepancy is 0.1766\%.
The capillary face-force difference is 2.0835\%, whereas the corresponding

\section{Implications for pore-scale simulation and supported scope}
The controlled mechanism shows how a higher-order interface term can alter
a low-rate capillary response while preserving measured width, leading
diffusion and pure-channel slip. With dynamic viscosity
$\mu_v=\rho_{ref}\nu=1/6$ in the present unit-reference-density setting,
its dimensionless strength, using capillary time $\mu_v H_p/\sigma$, is
\begin{equation}
 \Xi=\frac{\mu_v K_{\mathrm{num}}}{\sigma H_p^3}.
\end{equation}
The model also depends on $W_{0.9}/H_p$, $\lambda/H_p$ and $h/H_p$;
$\Xi$ alone is not an error criterion or a universal collapse.
The tested main surface tensions are .0025, .005 and .01.
Measured capillary numbers are of order $2\times10^{-4}$--$10^{-3}$ and
Reynolds numbers are below .003. Both low force and small interface
deformation are assumptions of the reduction.
The matched width ratio near .30 is deliberately diffuse and does not
constitute a sharp-interface regime.

The wall/bulk intervention establishes sensitivity in the specified
neutral closure. It does not prove equivalence of the complete moving
wall-phase boundary laws, measure a microscopic wetting length, or validate
nonneutral dynamic angles. Its additional reflected fourth-order boundary
conditions are numerical choices. Oblique interfaces, density contrast,
unequal viscosity, curved three-dimensional walls and pore-throat trapping
require separate tests; the coefficient derived for this projection map
must not be transferred to another phase algorithm.

Supporting calculations use the public 4.54~$\mu$m Bentheimer image
\cite{shah2014}. A periodic $48^3$ crop and its subdivided binary geometry
have independently compared single-phase LBPM permeability
\cite{mcclure2021}; those are numerical crosschecks of the specified image,
not laboratory validation. Its three-dimensional diffuse width is about
5.289 voxels, while the crop's median periodic distance to grain centers is
two original voxels. Continued two-phase and zero-force motion has not been
separated into physical interface relaxation and numerical currents.
Accordingly the rock fields and advective phase fluxes remain demonstrations
in the supplement. Steady relative permeability, resolved curved-rock
wetting and trapping thresholds are not results of this article.

The program includes raw-evidence/source identities, finite-state and
component-mass checks, adjacent-step convergence guards and reproducible
MPI/restart tests. Perturbation-kernel timings are retained
separately; they do not measure the present CSF path, cluster scaling or
GPU performance. The numerical diagnostics can guide a later pore-throat
study, but do not replace that physical validation.

\section{Conclusions}
The complete projection-phase stencil has measurable shape kinetics that
are not specified by its leading conservative Allen--Cahn equation and
normal-profile width. Its exact finite-grid spectrum predicts three-mode
and width-control full-solver decay without a fitted rate.
A conditional isolated-interface expansion supplies the transverse
fourth-order coefficient used in a selective numerical intervention.

That intervention explains most of the neutral-slit resistance gap over
three grids, with separate slip and surface-tension checks.
A low-force capillary/Stokes response reduction predicts the reference
resistance change and phase-volume shape without using the evolved
front to construct its operators. The left-null-vector relation identifies
how numerical relaxation can introduce surface-tension sensitivity in
steady mobility. Wall/bulk partitioning and complete force/velocity fields
bound the attribution more closely than a shared-stencil or mean-flow
match alone.

The result is a quantified numerical mechanism with reproducible program
support. It is not a new bulk multiphase model or a validated physical
contact-line law. Coupled manufactured consistency and the retained
reference-refinement limitations define what is established and what a
subsequent digital-rock physical validation must still resolve.
