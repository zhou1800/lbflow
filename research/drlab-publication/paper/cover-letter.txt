Dear Editor,

Please consider "Finite-interface transport and wetting effects in pore-scale lattice Boltzmann simulations with LBFlow" for the Computer Programs in Physics category of Computer Physics Communications.

The physical question is how diffuse numerical interfaces and wall treatment change pore-scale transport. Eighteen layered-flow calculations and three independent planar calibrations quantify arithmetic/harmonic constitutive bias, with sensitivity to continuum-reference reconstruction recorded separately. At the thickest tested interface and viscosity ratio 9:1, the flux shifts are -9.19% and +21.59%, largely reproduced by the independent continuum prediction. The conceptual separation and underlying layered-flow analysis have directly relevant prior work, including Lai et al. (2025), which is acknowledged explicitly.

Closed slit menisci measure signed capillary pressure at three widths and retain longer-hold checks. An experimental implementation of established curvature/Guo forcing reduces residual velocity while preserving a remaining pressure bias. Local nonlinear profile reconstruction is not uniformly better. Zero-surface-tension controls identify a wall-color distortion even at roundoff fluid velocity, motivating a more specific wall-segregation consistency question. The manuscript distinguishes these measured mechanisms from an unverified universal correction or trapping law.

The application is pore-scale flow on an attributed, openly licensed Bentheimer sandstone image. Analytic pore controls verify phase flux and viscosity normalization; an unmodified LBPM reference checks single-phase permeability on matching coarse and subdivided specimens. The two-phase cases retain their measured transient behavior and resolution limits, rather than infer an unverified relative-permeability curve. Dedicated repeated CPU/MPI timings separate numerical work from I/O and retain output identity across ranks.

The MIT-licensed program supports reproducible analysis of these physical effects, with corrected local collision and forcing, measured coupling checks and consistent MPI restart. The elementary continuum relation and the viscosity closures are not claimed as new physical laws. Capillary trapping thresholds remain unmeasured. The manuscript states the measured operating range, the distinction between source history and scientific priority, and AI-assisted work.

Before submission, public software access, an immutable release citation and required journal declarations must be verified. This draft does not attest originality, exclusive submission, authorship approval or publication rights on behalf of the author.
