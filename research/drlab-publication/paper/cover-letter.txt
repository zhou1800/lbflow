Dear Editor,

Please consider "Finite-interface transport and wetting effects in pore-scale lattice Boltzmann simulations with LBFlow" for the Computer Programs in Physics category of Computer Physics Communications.

The physical question is how numerical interface transport, wetting and capillary force change the conductance and displacement of immiscible fluids in a pore. Eighteen layered-flow controls distinguish diffuse constitutive bias from disagreement with independently reconstructed continuum references. At viscosity ratio 9:1, the thickest tested interfaces change flux by -9.19% and +21.59% for arithmetic and harmonic viscosity. The directly overlapping layered-flow and viscosity literature is acknowledged; neither the continuum balance nor these interpolation rules is claimed as a new law.

The continued study distinguishes a cosine-specific wall fixed point from the stationary balance already provided by published projection recoloring. An exact axial transport match compares core operators without fitting surface tension or capillary pressure. Conservative relative-phase wall transfer preserves the entire neutral transient. Nonneutral pressure, residual current and the known alternating lattice mode remain separately measured, with negative refinement results retained.

Guarded flat pressure reservoirs connect the measured closed capillary pressure to both directions of front drift, with exact component link-flux budgets and four-duration checks. Narrow pressure pairs remain bidirectional; estimated zero-drift pressure differs from closed pressure by at most 0.0066% of the Young reference. Front mobility is 11.3--17.8% below independently measured pure-channel mobility. A changed-physics zero-sigma control recovers the pure-channel response. These results do not establish a general contact-line law or a geometric throat trapping threshold.

A further 74 executions hold the independently measured interface-width ratio fixed and vary driving rate, channel length and duration with an unchanged numerical kernel. Doubling channel length at the coarse grid reduces the relative mobility deficit from 11.26% to 5.92%, while excess resistance changes by only 0.0333%. Actual capillary-force work, weighted by independent pure-channel fields, accounts for the incremental excess resistance within 0.023%. This identifies a localized interfacial response in the numerical momentum balance; three-grid deficits of 11.26%, 18.57% and 22.30% retain unresolved discretization and regularization effects. The known conservative Allen-Cahn behavior and its distinction from chemical-potential contact-line mobility are explicitly acknowledged. The reciprocal diagnostic is not presented as independent physical validation.

The MIT-licensed C++/MPI program, recorded executions and reproduction scripts support this bounded physical investigation. An attributed public Bentheimer image and unmodified LBPM single-phase comparisons provide the digital-rock application baseline; transient two-phase rock results do not establish relative-permeability curves. The manuscript documents operating limits, source provenance, CPU/MPI timings and AI-assisted work.

Public software access, an immutable release citation, author approval and required journal declarations must be finalized before submission. This draft does not attest exclusive submission or publication rights on behalf of the author.
