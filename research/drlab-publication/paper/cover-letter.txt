Dear Editor,

Please consider "Predicting numerical capillary resistance from discrete interface kinetics in LBFlow" for the Computer Programs in Physics category of Computer Physics Communications.

The manuscript investigates how a numerical interface changes low-rate capillary transport even after width, leading diffusion and hydraulic slip are matched. The contribution is a quantitative numerical mechanism, with a reproducible C++/MPI program and independent analysis tools, rather than a claim of a new bulk multiphase model.

An exact projection-phase spectrum, including unit-normal variation, predicts decay in 26 full-solver trajectories within 0.00084%, spanning three transverse modes and two widths. A conditional isolated-interface expansion supplies the transverse fourth-order coefficient used in a selective intervention. Adding this term alone to an independent Stokes/finite-volume reference reduces the three-grid neutral-slit speed gaps from 17.12%, 15.18% and 13.72% to 1.023%, 0.452% and 0.275%, with a separately prescribed larger slip checked.

The new low-force capillary/Stokes response reduction uses no evolving front data to construct its operators. It predicts the full reference mean speeds within 0.0564% across the available slip/grid/strength/surface-tension comparisons. Its left-null-vector relation explains how numerical shape relaxation can introduce surface-tension sensitivity into steady mobility. Conservative wall/bulk interventions test where that response is sensitive, without inferring a molecular wall length.

Continuous-source coupled manufactured tests verify near-second-order phase and velocity convergence. Full recorded force/velocity fields distinguish hydraulic error from phase evolution. The primary scope is neutral equal-viscosity D2Q9 flow; physical moving-contact-line validation, curved-rock wetting and steady rock relative permeability are not claimed. An attributed digital-rock demonstration and the older recovery/performance evidence are retained in the supplement.

The MIT-licensed software, frozen source identities, raw-evidence manifest, relocated reanalysis, operator derivations and MPI/restart controls accompany the study. Prior conservative Allen-Cahn, phase-field/profile-preservation, wetting and slip developments are acknowledged, including the closely related 2024 color-gradient-based phase-field formulation. AI-assisted work is disclosed.

Public software access, an immutable program-archive citation, author review and final submission attestations must be finalized before submission. This draft does not attest exclusive submission or publication rights on behalf of the author.
