Dear Editor,

Please consider "LBFlow: verification-guided MPI simulation of two-phase flow in digital rock" for the Computer Programs in Physics category of Computer Physics Communications.

The accompanying implementation and experiments make collision, forcing, interface and restart behavior inspectable. Reproducible counterexamples identify defects in a historical solver, including fixed first-phase MRT relaxation and incompatible momentum forcing. The corrected implementation recovers analytic acceleration and channel flow, while layered-flow and Laplace tests quantify its multiphase operating range. A wall-stencil ablation reports both contact-angle improvements and increased spurious flow in some cases.

The application is pore-scale flow on an attributed, openly licensed Bentheimer sandstone image. Analytic pore controls verify phase flux and viscosity normalization; an unmodified LBPM reference checks single-phase permeability on matching coarse and subdivided specimens. The two-phase cases retain their measured transient behavior and resolution limits, rather than infer an unverified relative-permeability curve. Dedicated repeated CPU/MPI timings separate numerical work from I/O and retain output identity across ranks.

The contribution is an inspectable MIT-licensed program, physical coupling checks and a reproducible digital-rock workflow. The manuscript states the measured operating range, the distinction between source history and scientific priority, and AI-assisted work.

Before submission, public software access, an immutable release citation and required journal declarations must be verified. This draft does not attest originality, exclusive submission, authorship approval or publication rights on behalf of the author.
